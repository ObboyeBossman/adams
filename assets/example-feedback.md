# Example Feedback — Three Delivered Closes

> **This file is the worked example for the close, in coach voice.** Each
> example below is the actual text a coach would deliver to a student
> after a rep — second person, direct, not meta-description. They are
> written to be read alongside `references/session-evaluation.md` (which
> defines the close contract and the five movements) and
> `references/conversation-evaluation-criteria.md` (which defines the
> scoring rubric and sequencing). The three examples cover the three
> signature situations: a warmth-heavy collapse (Branson-heavy,
> Shelby-absent), a frame-heavy collapse (Shelby-heavy, Branson-absent),
> and a genuine blend moment scored honestly as a moment, not yet a
> capacity. The third example uses the canonical student M—— at S-016,
> interlocking with `assets/example-session-log.md`.

---

## Example 1 — Branson-Heavy, Shelby-Absent

> **Scenario.** Student: K——, three weeks into coaching, naturally warm
> fundraising professional. Rep: a major donor asked K—— to redirect
> restricted funds to a different program; K—— agreed in the room,
> contrary to policy, then confessed the agreement in the debrief. The
> rep exercised warmth, frame, composure, and reading the other person.
> This close is delivered after the debrief.

> **Coach:**
>
> Let me tell you what I saw in the rep, and then what I saw in the
> debrief, because they are different stories and both matter.
>
> In the rep: you opened with genuine warmth — you remembered his
> daughter's name, you asked about the marathon, and the question was
> real, not performed. He felt seen. That is your strength, and it
> showed. When he made the ask, you listened fully before responding —
> you did not interrupt, you did not brace. That listening is the
> Branson pole, and it is competent.
>
> Then he pressed, and you agreed to something you cannot deliver. In
> the debrief, you told me you knew it was wrong as you said it. That
> is the data I want to score.
>
> **Composure: 5.** Your pace did not accelerate when he pressed, and
> your pitch did not rise. That is competent. What kept it from a 6:
> the composure was the composure of accommodation, not the composure
> of held ground. There was no flinch because there was no resistance.
> Composure under pressure is different from composure under
> agreement; the pressure never landed because you gave before it
> could. The 5 reflects: calm in the room, but the calm was not
> tested.
>
> **Frame Control: 2.** When he said "I'd rather see it go to the
> schools program," you said "I think we can make that work" — and you
> said it within half a second, without pause, without checking
> policy, without naming that the restriction was the donor's at
> gift-time and cannot be redirected unilaterally. The frame collapsed
> entirely. Sequencing check: composure was 5, so frame above 4 was
> available. The 2 is evidence, not a sequencing cap — your composure
> was adequate; the frame simply was not there. What kept it from a 3:
> you knew it was wrong as you said it, which means the collapse was
> not ignorance but a reflex.
>
> **Genuine Warmth & Attention: 6.** The opening was real, the
> attention was on the person, and the follow-up question proved the
> listening. This is your strongest criterion and it is genuinely
> strong. What kept it from a 7: the warmth was offered without
> ground underneath it — warmth without frame is not the Branson mode,
> it is the people-pleaser corruption of Branson mode. Branson's
> warmth is real *because* his frame is real; the warmth is a choice
> made from ground, not a reflex made from the need to be liked. Your
> warmth is real; the ground it stands on is not yet there. The 6
> reflects: real warmth, missing the frame that would make it
> Branson-grade.
>
> **The Blend: 2.** Sequencing check: blend requires both poles at 6.
> Warmth was 6; frame was 2. The blend is not available; the 2
> reflects that no integration was possible because one pole was
> absent. This is not a failure of integration; it is a failure of one
> of the poles. The blend is not the work yet. Frame is the work.
>
> The single load-bearing gap is your **frame control** — not because
> it was your lowest score (it was), but because frame is the missing
> ground under your warmth. Your warmth is real and competent. Without
> frame, that warmth will keep costing you: you will keep agreeing in
> the room and confessing in the debrief, and the donors will
> eventually notice that your yes does not hold. Closing the frame
> gap is what makes your warmth safe to deploy. It is the ground
> under your gift.
>
> One rep, this week, in a real conversation: when someone makes an
> ask you cannot immediately say yes or no to, the only behaviour is
> "let me check and get back to you by [specific time]." That is the
> whole rep. Not the no — that comes later. Not the explanation —
> that comes later. Just the pause, and the commitment to return.
> One conversation is enough. We will see what it does to the frame
> score next session.
>
> Trajectory: flat. Three sessions in, composure has held at 5,
> warmth has held at 6, frame has held at 2. This is not failure —
> three sessions is not long enough to expect movement on a gap this
> deep. But flat is not progress, and I am telling you it is flat so
> you do not mistake the warmth score for arrival. The story of the
> next period is whether the frame moves. If it does not move in two
> more sessions, we re-diagnose; the gap may be upstream of a deeper
> discomfort with disappointing people, which is character work, not
> just skill work. We will know in two sessions.

## Example 2 — Shelby-Heavy, Branson-Absent

> **Scenario.** Student: D——, four months into coaching, senior
> engineer, naturally still and composed. Rep: a junior engineer
> missed a deadline for the third time; D—— delivered the hard
> feedback in a real conversation yesterday and brought the exchange
> to today's session for review. The junior engineer left the
> conversation visibly deflated and submitted their resignation
> inquiry the same afternoon. The rep exercised composure, frame,
> warmth, hard-truth delivery, and the blend.

> **Coach:**
>
> Let me tell you what I saw in the exchange you brought.
>
> You opened with the missed deadline, named it factually, named the
> pattern (this is the third time), named the consequence (the
> release slipped), and stated the expectation (this cannot continue).
> The junior tried to explain, and you held the frame: you did not
> let the conversation become about the explanations, you returned it
> to the pattern. That frame-hold is real, and it is the strongest
> thing in the exchange. Then the junior went quiet, and you stayed
> in the frame — you did not soften, you did not rescue, you let the
> silence sit. That is Shelby-grade stillness, and it is competent.
>
> And the junior left the conversation and inquired about resigning
> the same afternoon. That is the data I want to score.
>
> **Composure: 7.** Pace did not accelerate, pitch did not rise, the
> silence was held without visible discomfort. The composure survived
> the junior's visible distress — you did not flinch when they went
> quiet, and you did not rush to fill the silence. What kept it from
> an 8: the composure had a quality of *withholding* rather than
> *presence* — you were not rattled, but you were also not fully
> there. A 9 has the composure of someone who is wholly present in
> the difficult moment, not someone who is holding themselves out of
> it. The 7 is honest: the composure held; the presence did not.
>
> **Frame Control: 7.** The frame-hold on the pattern was clean
> across multiple turns. The junior tried to make it about the
> explanations, the workload, the unclear spec; you returned it to
> the pattern each time, in one sentence, without defensiveness.
> Sequencing check: composure was 7, so frame above 4 was available;
> the 7 is evidence, not capped. What kept it from an 8: the frame
> was held *against* the junior, not *for* the work. A 9 holds frame
> in a way that the other person can feel is in service of the work,
> not in opposition to them. Your frame was technically correct and
> relationally cold. The junior felt the frame as a wall, not as a
> standard.
>
> **Genuine Warmth & Attention: 3.** You did not ask a single
> question about the junior's situation. You did not check what was
> going on underneath the missed deadlines — and three missed
> deadlines in a row, from someone with a previously clean record, is
> a signal that something has changed, and the something is almost
> always personal. The junior was a person before they were a
> performance problem, and you treated them only as the latter. What
> kept it from a 2: you were not cruel. You were accurate. But
> accuracy without attention is not warmth; it is indifference
> wearing the clothes of professionalism. The 3 reflects: no
> cruelty, no warmth.
>
> **Delivering Hard Truth: 4.** Sequencing check: hard truth at the
> highest tier requires the blend at 6 in the delivery moment. The
> blend was not at 6 (see below); the hard-truth score is capped at 5
> by sequencing. The 4 is evidence, under the cap: the truth was
> delivered accurately and held, but it did not land. The junior
> received it as a verdict, not as information they could act on.
> Hard truth that lands requires the recipient to feel that the
> deliverer is for them even as the message is hard; without the
> warmth, the message becomes a wall.
>
> **The Blend: 3.** Sequencing check: blend requires both poles at 6.
> Composure was 7; warmth was 3. Blend not available; the 3 is
> evidence, capped by the warmth prerequisite. You held the Shelby
> pole competently and omitted the Branson pole entirely. This is
> the cold-operator corruption of Shelby mode — frame without warmth,
> respected and walked away from.
>
> The single load-bearing gap is your **warmth** — specifically the
> *receiving* dimension, the capacity to be curious about the person
> in front of you even when you are holding them to account. Not
> because warmth is your lowest score (it is), but because the warmth
> is what would have made the hard truth land. You have the frame to
> hold any line. You are missing the warmth that would let the line
> be received. The junior did not resign because your frame was too
> hard; the junior resigned because the frame came without any
> evidence that you saw them as a person. Closing the warmth gap is
> what makes your hard-truth delivery usable. Without it, you will
> keep delivering hard truths that are correct and that cost you the
> people you deliver them to.
>
> One rep, this week, in a real conversation with someone you
> supervise: before you deliver any feedback — positive or negative —
> ask one question about the person. Not about the work; about the
> person. "How are you doing, actually?" is enough. Then listen to
> the answer. Then deliver the feedback. One conversation is enough.
> We will see what it does to the warmth score, and to the
> hard-truth score, next session.
>
> Trajectory: regressing. Four months in, composure has held at 7
> across the last four sessions (steady). Frame has held at 7 across
> the last three (steady, improving from 6). Warmth has dropped from
> 5 to 4 to 3 across the last three sessions — and this is the
> regression I want to name honestly. The Shelby training has been
> landing; that is real, and the composure and frame scores reflect
> it. But the Shelby training has been landing *at the cost of* the
> warmth, which is the opposite of what we are building. The dual
> benchmark is both poles; you are strengthening one and losing the
> other. This is the corruption the Shelby pole is most vulnerable
> to, and it is the corruption I should have caught two sessions ago.
> The story of the next period is whether the warmth can be
> recovered without losing the frame. If it cannot, we re-diagnose;
> the gap may be that the warmth and the frame are competing for
> your bandwidth rather than integrating, which is a different
> problem and a different intervention.

## Example 3 — Genuine Blend Moment, Scored as a Moment

> **Scenario.** Student: M——, eight weeks into coaching, the
> canonical people-pleaser from `conversation-evaluation-criteria.md`,
> `session-evaluation.md`, `logging-template.md`, and the full S-014
> through S-016 arc in `assets/example-session-log.md`. This is the
> close for S-016. The rep: a real upcoming salary conversation with
> M——'s manager, rehearsed in Phase 4 with the coach playing the
> manager. In the third exchange of the rehearsal, M—— produced a
> genuine blend moment — warm opening directed at the person, frame
> held on the number, warmth running *through* the frame-hold rather
> than tagged onto it. This is the close that follows that rep.

> **Coach:**
>
> Let me tell you what I saw.
>
> You opened the rehearsal with the composure pause — held it under
> your manager's framing of the budget constraint, returned to your
> number without acceleration. That is the composure you lost in
> S-015 under the senior load, and it is back. Two sessions ago, that
> pause disappeared the moment your manager pushed; today, it held
> across three exchanges with varying pressure. That is real, and
> you should register it.
>
> Then, in the third exchange, you did something you have not done
> before in our work. Your manager pushed on the budget, and you
> said: "I understand the budget is tight, and I want to be
> reasonable about the timing. I'm not going to move off the number,
> because the number reflects the scope, and the scope hasn't
> changed. I'd rather find a path on the timing than on the number."
>
> That is the blend. The warmth ("I understand the budget is tight, I
> want to be reasonable") was real, not performed. The frame ("I'm
> not going to move off the number") was unconditional. And the
> warmth ran *through* the frame-hold — it was not a tag at the
> beginning and the firmness at the end. The whole turn was both at
> once. That is the first time I have seen you produce that.
>
> **Composure: 7.** Pause held across all three exchanges, including
> the budget-pressure exchange. Pace did not accelerate; pitch did
> not rise. What kept it from an 8: the pause is still slightly
> effortful — I can see you choosing it. A 9 has no effortful
> quality; the pause is just there, as a feature of how you operate.
> The 7 is the same 7 you held in S-014, recovered from S-015's 4.
> Recovery is real; ownership continues.
>
> **Frame Control: 6.** The number was held across all three
> exchanges, including the exchange where the manager tried to
> reframe it as a timing issue. You returned the conversation to the
> scope, which is where the number lives. Sequencing check: composure
> was 7, so frame above 4 was available; the 6 is evidence. What
> kept it from a 7: the frame was held reactively — you held it
> *against* the pressure, in response to the manager's pushes. A 7
> holds frame proactively, shaping the conversation before the
> pressure arrives. You are still responding to the frame rather
> than setting it. That is the next work on frame, when we return
> to it.
>
> **Genuine Warmth & Attention: 6.** The opening of the third
> exchange — "I understand the budget is tight, and I want to be
> reasonable about the timing" — was real warmth directed at the
> person and the situation, not at the request. It was not
> performed; it was not a softener. It was a genuine acknowledgment
> that the manager has constraints, offered without conceding the
> number. Moved from 5. What kept it from a 7: the warmth appeared
> in the third exchange, after two exchanges of all-frame. A 7 has
> the warmth present from the first turn, running through the whole
> conversation. The 6 reflects: warmth present and real, not yet
> pervasive.
>
> **The Blend: 5.** Sequencing check: blend requires both poles at 6
> and one integration moment. Composure 7, warmth 6 — prerequisites
> satisfied. One integration moment produced in the third exchange.
> A blend score up to 6 was available by sequencing. The 5 reflects
> evidence, not a cap: the moment was real, and the capacity is not
> yet owned. I want to be very plain about this, because this is the
> place where inflation is most tempting and most costly. You
> produced a blend moment. You have not yet produced a blend
> capacity. A capacity is a behaviour that has been demonstrated
> under load, repeatedly, across contexts. A moment is a behaviour
> that has been demonstrated once, in a specific rep, in a
> rehearsal. The 5 reflects: moment achieved, capacity not yet
> owned. The next two sessions are about whether the moment repeats.
> If it repeats under different pressure, in a real conversation,
> the score moves. If it does not repeat, the score holds, and we
> learn where the moment was specific to the rehearsal. Both
> outcomes are honest; both are useful. I will not round this up to
> 6 to celebrate the breakthrough. The breakthrough is real, and I
> have named it. The score reflects the capacity.
>
> The single load-bearing gap is the **blend — integration under
> varied pressure.** Not because it is your lowest score (it is, at
> 5), but because the moment has revealed that the integration is
> now possible, which means it is now the highest-leverage work. The
> composure is adequate. The frame is adequate. The warmth is
> adequate. None of them is owned at the ceiling, but all three are
> sufficient to support the blend. The blend is the target — it is
> the highest expression of the dual benchmark and it is what all
> eight weeks of work has been building toward. The leverage is the
> whole benchmark. This is the work now.
>
> One rep, this week, ideally in the real salary conversation with
> your manager — the one we just rehearsed. The assignment: produce
> the blend moment again, intentionally this time. Warm opening
> directed at the person, then frame-hold on the number, with the
> warmth running through the frame-hold. If it does not appear, that
> is data — note what was different about the real conversation that
> pulled you out of it. If it appears, note what was the same. One
> conversation; one attempt; observe. We will see whether the moment
> becomes a capacity.
>
> Trajectory: improving. Composure recovered from S-015's 4 back to
> 7; the regression was real and is now recovered, not erased.
> Warmth moved from 5 to 6 for the first time in three sessions.
> Blend moved from 4 to 5 on the strength of the integration
> moment. The story of this period: the regression in S-015
> revealed a gap that was always there — composure under authority
> pressure — and S-016 closed it and produced a moment of the blend.
> The story of the next period: whether the moment becomes a
> capacity. I am telling you improving, not arriving. You are not
> there yet. You are closer than you were, and the path is visible,
> and the next two sessions will tell us whether the path is being
> walked or only glimpsed.

## What These Three Examples Have in Common

Read together, the three examples show the close contract holding
across three very different situations:

- **Observation grounds the scores.** In each example, the scores
  begin with a concrete description of what the student did, not with
  an evaluation. The student hears the behaviour before they hear the
  number.
- **Scores respect sequencing.** In Example 1, the blend is capped at
  2 because the frame was absent. In Example 2, the hard-truth score
  is capped at 5 (and scored at 4 on evidence) because the blend was
  not at 6. In Example 3, the blend is scored at 5, not 6, because a
  moment is not a capacity. In each case, the sequencing cap is named
  to the student, not hidden.
- **The single load-bearing gap is named with leverage.** In Example
  1, the gap is frame, because frame is the ground under the warmth.
  In Example 2, the gap is warmth, because warmth is what would make
  the hard truth land. In Example 3, the gap is the blend, because
  the moment has revealed the integration is now possible. In each
  case, the gap is the highest-leverage work, not the most visible
  failure.
- **The next rep assignment is concrete and reppable in one real
  conversation.** Not "work on frame" — "say 'let me check and get
  back to you by [time]' when you cannot immediately answer." Not
  "work on warmth" — "ask one question about the person before
  delivering any feedback." Not "produce the blend" — "produce the
  blend moment again, in the real salary conversation, and observe
  what is different."
- **The trajectory sentence is honest and evidence-anchored.**
  Example 1 is flat (three sessions in, no movement yet). Example 2
  is regressing (warmth dropping as Shelby training lands). Example 3
  is improving (composure recovered, warmth moved, blend moment
  produced). In each case, the trajectory call is separated from the
  current score and is cited against prior-session data.

## What Is Different Across the Three Examples

- **Tone.** Example 1 is warm-strict: the student is early in
  coaching and the warmth in the coach's voice models the warmth the
  student already has. Example 2 is colder and more direct: the
  student is four months in, the regression is on the coach as much
  as the student, and the tone names that. Example 3 is warm-strict
  again, but with the breakthrough-handling discipline visible — the
  warmth in the coach's voice names the moment; the strictness in
  the coach's voice refuses to inflate it.
- **Gap diagnosis.** Example 1's gap is upstream of the visible
  failure (frame, not the agreement). Example 2's gap is the
  corruption of the student's strength (warmth, lost as Shelby
  training landed). Example 3's gap is the integration itself (the
  blend, now possible because the poles are adequate). Each gap is
  the highest-leverage work for that student at that moment.
- **Rep dose.** Example 1's rep is small (one pause-and-return), for
  an early student whose gap is deep. Example 2's rep is small (one
  question about the person), for a regressing student whose
  bandwidth is consumed by the regression. Example 3's rep is larger
  (produce the blend again, in a real conversation), for an
  improving student whose capacity is beginning to consolidate. The
  standard does not move; the dose flexes to the student.

This is what coaching sounds like when the close contract is held.
The voice flexes; the structure does not. The scores flex to the
evidence; the sequencing does not. The gap flexes to the leverage;
the one-gap rule does not. The rep flexes to the dose the student
can absorb; the reppability does not. The trajectory flexes to the
arc; the honesty does not. Standards fixed, methods flexible — the
governing principle, heard in the coach's actual voice.

---

*The close is the truth, told in love, with a path. These three
examples are what that sounds like. Rep them; do not recite them.
The student in front of you is not the student in these examples,
and the close that serves them is the one their rep, their
evidence, their gap, and their arc require — built fresh, every
session, on the structure that does not move.*
