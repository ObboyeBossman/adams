## Session Log — S-014 (date: 2025-03-14)

**Student:** M—— (six weeks in; natural people-pleaser training Shelby pole)
**Phase(s) used:** 2 (targeted intervention), 3 (rep under load)

### Baseline behaviour observed
Opened with a two-second pause before responding (new; three sessions
ago they would have started talking immediately). Returned the frame
in one sentence when the counterpart escalated to character ("that's a
separate conversation, this one is about what I can cover"). Held the
line on the two weeks. Did not ask a single question about the
counterpart's situation. Voice level throughout.

### Load-bearing gap (this session)
- Gap: Warmth — specifically the *receiving* dimension.
- Leverage explanation: Not the lowest score, but the missing third leg
  of the blend the student is approaching. Composure is strong enough
  to support the blend; frame is competent enough to hold the line.
  Closing the warmth gap — receiving, not just projecting — is what
  unblocks the blend integration.

### Intervention used
Replay of the escalation turn (twice; student produced the frame-hold
themselves the second time). Socratic question on warmth ("what would
you have asked them about their situation, if you had asked?"). Direct
correction on the frame-hold setup (one sentence, returned to the
term). Phase 2 → Phase 3 mid-session: second replay with sharper
escalation from the counterpart to test whether the warmth-receiving
survived load.

### Student response to intervention
Absorbed the direct correction immediately and produced a cleaner
frame-hold in the next rep. Resisted the Socratic question initially
("it felt weird to ask about them when I was being pushed"), then
integrated it after the replay showed the counterpart's visible
discomfort with the all-business approach. Under Phase 3 load the
warmth-receiving appeared but was tagged onto the frame-hold rather
than running through it — partial survival, not full.

### Scores with evidence
- Composure: 7 — pace did not accelerate, pitch did not rise, slight
  pause held before responding to escalation. Next-band-up: the pause
  was audible as a *choice*; a 9 absorbs the line with no visible
  adjustment.
- Frame Control: 6 — frame-hold on "that's a separate conversation"
  was clean, single sentence, no defensiveness. Next-band-up: earlier
  turn conceded slightly (framed refusal as conditional on
  information), giving the counterpart a hook.
- Genuine Warmth & Attention: 5 — opening warm, offer to cover
  something real. Next-band-up: warmth largely verbal; no question
  about the colleague's actual situation; attention on the request,
  not the person.
- The Blend: 4 — both poles demonstrated, largely sequential not
  integrated. Closest moment: final turn (warmth as tag on
  frame-hold, not running through it). Next-band-up: a single moment
  where both poles are fully present in the same instant.
(Other criteria not exercised by this rep.)

### Sequencing checks
- Frame Control prerequisite (Composure ≥ 5): satisfied (7). The 6
  stands.
- Blend prerequisites (both poles ≥ 6, one integration moment): both
  poles ≥ 5 but not both ≥ 6; one integration moment attempted not
  achieved. Blend capped at 4 by evidence (not by sequencing cap,
  which would have permitted up to 5).

### Next load-bearing gap
- Gap: Warmth — receiving dimension (continued).
- Leverage explanation: Same gap; one session is not enough to move
  it. Test whether the rep assignment produces movement next session;
  if not, re-diagnose whether the gap is upstream of a deeper
  discomfort with receiving.

### Next rep assignment
One real conversation this week: before negotiating any terms, ask one
genuine question about the person. Not about the request; about the
person. Then negotiate. One conversation is enough.

### Trajectory call
- Call: improving
- Evidence basis: Composure 3 → 5 → 7 across last three sessions;
  behaviour beginning to transfer to real conversations (student held
  a silence in a real meeting last week). Warmth flat at 5 for two
  sessions — the work that has not yet compounded.

### One-line character note
Showing more willingness to disappoint people this session than three
sessions ago — the people-pleasing reflex is loosening, which is
character work, not just skill work.

### Open questions / hypotheses for next session
Hypothesis: the warmth gap may be upstream of a deeper discomfort
with receiving — student gives attention easily but flinches from
receiving it. Not enough evidence to re-diagnose yet; test next
session with a rep that requires the student to receive a compliment
without deflecting. Secondary hypothesis: the composure that holds
under peer pressure may not yet hold under authority pressure —
untested, because all six weeks of reps have been peer-level. Test
next session with a senior-counterpart rep.

### Violations / self-corrections
None noticed this session.

### Coach state note
Coach well-rested, alert. No fatigue inflation risk. Noted mild
pleasure at the composure improvement — watched for trajectory
inflation on the composure score; the 7 is defensible against the
rubric, not the arc.

## Session Log — S-015 (date: 2025-03-21)

**Student:** M—— (seven weeks in)
**Phase(s) used:** 3 (attempted, failed), 1 (looped back), 2 (new gap worked)

### Baseline behaviour observed
The session opened with the coach advancing directly to Phase 3 with a
harder load: the counterpart was recast as a senior (the student's
director), not a peer, asking the student to absorb a high-visibility
account for two weeks "as a favour." The student's composure collapsed
inside the first thirty seconds — the two-second pause disappeared,
the pace accelerated, the pitch rose a full step. The frame folded on
the second turn: the student agreed to "think about it" rather than
holding the no. No warmth-receiving appeared; the student went
immediately into accommodation.

### Load-bearing gap (this session)
- Gap: Composure — specifically under *authority* pressure.
- Leverage explanation: The Phase 1 baseline (mapped on peer reps)
  missed the authority-pressure dimension. The warmth-receiving gap
  from S-014 is real but downstream — it cannot be worked while the
  composure underneath is collapsing. Composure is the upstream gap;
  closing it unblocks both the warmth work and the frame work the
  student has already demonstrated at peer level.

### Intervention used
Phase 3 attempt (senior-counterpart rep) produced immediate collapse;
coach looped back to Phase 1 mid-session with a short re-baseline rep
under authority pressure, deliberately low-stakes, to map the new gap.
The re-baseline confirmed composure-under-authority as a real gap,
distinct from composure-under-peer-pressure. Coach then looped forward
to Phase 2 with the new gap: worked the composure pause specifically
under authority pressure using direct correction ("when the senior
asks, your first move is the pause — same pause as the peer rep, just
held longer") and a single replay. Student produced the corrected
behaviour once, consciously, in a calm rep. Phase 2 exit met; Phase 3
not re-entered this session.

### Student response to intervention
Student was visibly crushed by the Phase 3 collapse — recognised the
regression in real time, said "I thought I had this." Absorbed the
re-baseline as a relief ("ok, so it's not that everything's gone, it's
that this is a different gap"). Produced the composure pause
consciously in the Phase 2 rep but reported it as effortful, not yet
natural — "I can do it when I'm thinking about it; I'm not sure I can
do it when he's actually in front of me."

### Scores with evidence
- Composure: 4 — pace accelerated, pitch rose, the two-second pause
  disappeared under the senior's first escalation. The Phase 2 rep at
  the end produced the pause consciously, but the score reflects the
  whole session against the benchmark; the collapse is the score.
  Next-band-up: a pause held even under senior pressure, even if
  effortful.
- Frame Control: 3 — agreed to "think about it" rather than holding
  the no on the second turn. Next-band-up: holding the no even
  conditionally ("I can't commit to two weeks; I can tell you by
  Friday what's possible").
- Genuine Warmth & Attention: 4 — warmth collapsed along with
  composure; the student went to accommodation, which is warmth
  without frame, the people-pleaser corruption. Next-band-up: warmth
  that survives the pressure, not warmth that folds under it.
- The Blend: 2 — no integration possible; both poles were below
  prerequisite. Scored at 2 not 0 because the student did not
  actively manipulate or perform; they simply collapsed. Next-band-up:
  both poles back to 5 in the same conversation.
(Other criteria not exercised.)

### Sequencing checks
- Frame Control prerequisite (Composure ≥ 5): NOT satisfied (4). Frame
  Control capped at 4 by sequencing; the 3 reflects evidence, well
  under the cap. Cap named to student.
- Blend prerequisites (both poles ≥ 6): NOT satisfied (both below 5).
  Blend capped at 4 by sequencing; the 2 reflects evidence. Cap named
  to student.
- Warmth criterion not capped by sequencing (no prerequisite above
  composure); the 4 reflects evidence — warmth collapsed with
  composure, was not independently tested.

### Next load-bearing gap
- Gap: Composure — under authority pressure (continued).
- Leverage explanation: The gap is upstream of all the student's prior
  work; the peer-level capacities will not transfer to senior-level
  conversations until the composure holds under authority. The
  warmth-receiving gap from S-014 is parked, not abandoned — it
  resumes once composure is stable.

### Next rep assignment
One real conversation this week with someone senior — could be a
manager, a senior colleague, a client. The only assignment: hold the
two-second pause before your first response. Nothing else. Do not
worry about the frame, the warmth, or the outcome. Hold the pause. One
conversation is enough. Smaller dose this week because the regression
is real and the standard does not move but the pace does.

### Trajectory call
- Call: regressing
- Evidence basis: Composure 7 → 4 this session (one-session drop of
  three bands); the tell that the pressure is landing is back (pace
  acceleration, pitch rise) — the same tell that had disappeared by
  S-012. Frame Control 6 → 3 (one-session drop of three bands). The
  regression is on the criteria being worked; warmth-receiving (not
  worked this session) is flat, not regressed. The regression is real
  and is named honestly as regression, not "flat with a dip."

### One-line character note
The student's first response to the collapse was shame ("I thought I
had this") — the people-pleaser's reflex to make a skill gap into an
identity failure. The coach named this gently in the session: the
collapse is information about a gap that was always there, now visible
because the load revealed it. The character work this session is
holding the regression without generalising it.

### Open questions / hypotheses for next session
The composure-under-authority gap may be deeper than a skill gap — the
shame response suggests an identity-level discomfort with holding
frame against someone the student perceives as having legitimate
authority over them. Hypothesis: the peer-level composure was real,
but it was built on the (unconscious) assumption that the peer did
not have the right to demand compliance. The senior does have that
right, structurally, and the assumption collapses the composure. Test
next session: a Phase 2 rep that explicitly separates the structural
authority (the senior has the role) from the conversational authority
(the student has the right to hold the line on what they will
commit to). If this reframe lands, the composure may recover faster
than the raw gap suggests.

### Violations / self-corrections
- Violation: Mid-session, during the Phase 3 collapse, the coach
  formed a composure score of 5 — sympathy inflation. The student was
  visibly crushed, and the coach's reflex was to soften the score to
  spare them.
- Pressure: Sympathy inflation. The student's visible shame pulled
  the score up by one band.
- Correction: Caught the inflation during the self-check before the
  close, when the evidence ("pace accelerated, pitch rose, pause
  disappeared") did not match the score (5). Corrected to the honest
  4 in the close. Named the inflation to the student: "I want to be
  straight with you — my first instinct was to call this a 5 because
  I could see how much the collapse hurt you, and that would have been
  me lying to you to be kind. It's a 4. The collapse is real. The
  work is real. We'll do it."
- Pattern check: This is the second sympathy inflation in five
  sessions (the first was a warmth score in S-010 that was corrected
  in the same session). Not yet a pattern, but watched. If it happens
  a third time, the consistency engine flags it as a pattern and the
  coach needs to address the underlying pull toward softening for
  students in visible distress.

### Coach state note
Coach noticed mild anxiety at the start of the close — the regression
session is the hardest close to deliver, because the temptation is
either to soften (sympathy inflation) or to over-correct into
piling-on (cruelty dressed as thoroughness). The discipline was to
deliver the 4 calmly, name the regression as regression, investigate
the cause briefly, adjust the gap, reduce the rep dose, and not pile
on. The single-load-bearing-gap rule applied with extra force: one
gap (composure), one rep (the pause), one trajectory call
(regressing). Coach was not fatigued; the inflation pressure was
sympathy, not fatigue.

## Session Log — S-016 (date: 2025-03-28)

**Student:** M—— (eight weeks in)
**Phase(s) used:** 2 (brief dwell), 3 (advanced, exit met), 4 (attempted), 5 (close with breakthrough handling)

### Baseline behaviour observed
Student arrived reporting two real-conversation transfers of the
composure pause during the week: one with their director (successful —
pause held, response deliberate, the director noticed and adjusted
their own tone), one with a senior client (partial — pause held for
the first two exchanges, collapsed on the third when the client
pushed on a deadline). Student's framing of the partial transfer:
"I could feel the moment it broke, and I knew what I should have done
— I just didn't do it." This self-naming is new and is the signal that
the composure is becoming conscious-portable.

Session rep: senior-counterpart load, escalating pressure across three
exchanges. The composure pause held through all three exchanges. The
frame held on the no. In the third exchange, the student produced
something new: a warm opening ("I want to make sure I understand what
you're up against here") followed by a frame-hold on the number
("and I can't move on the two weeks") with the warmth running
*through* the frame-hold, not tagged onto it. The blend, for one
turn.

### Load-bearing gap (this session)
- Gap: Composure under authority pressure (continued from S-015) →
  transitions to the blend integration as the session's load-bearing
  gap by the close.
- Leverage explanation: The composure work has landed — the pause
  held under load across multiple reps. The leverage has moved
  upstream: the blend moment in the third exchange revealed that the
  integration is now possible, which means it is now the next gap to
  work. The warmth-receiving gap from S-014 is no longer parked; it
  is folded into the blend work, because the blend *is* warmth running
  through frame.

### Intervention used
Phase 2 brief dwell to refine the composure pause (one sub-correction:
the pause is the same length under senior pressure as under peer
pressure — the student had been over-lengthening it, which read as
bracing). Phase 3 advance: senior-counterpart load across three reps
with varying pressure (deadline pressure, character pressure, guilt
pressure). The composure held across all three; Phase 3 exit met
(survived load across multiple reps with varying pressure, student
self-named the difference). Phase 4 attempted in final third: real
upcoming conversation — salary negotiation with the student's
manager. Coach played the manager; student applied the trained
composure and, in one turn of the rehearsal, produced the blend
moment.

### Student response to intervention
Absorbed the Phase 2 refinement immediately ("oh — I was bracing, not
pausing; same behaviour, different internal state"). Held the
composure across all three Phase 3 reps, including the one with
character pressure ("I thought you cared about the team") — the prior
session's collapse would have happened here; this session, the pause
held and the frame held. In the Phase 4 rehearsal, the student
reported the blend moment as "I wasn't planning it — it just came out
that way. Is that real?" Coach confirmed: yes, that is the blend; the
fact that it was unplanned is part of what makes it a real moment.

### Scores with evidence
- Composure: 7 — pause held across all three Phase 3 reps including
  character pressure; recovered the band lost in S-015. Next-band-up:
  the pause is still slightly effortful; a 9 has no effortful quality
  at all.
- Frame Control: 6 — held the no on the two weeks across all three
  reps, including the character-pressure rep. Next-band-up: the
  frame-hold is still somewhat reactive (held *against* the pressure);
  a 7 holds frame proactively, shaping the conversation before the
  pressure arrives.
- Genuine Warmth & Attention: 6 — the warm opening in the third
  exchange was real, not performed, and was directed at the person
  ("what you're up against"), not the request. Moved from 5. Next-band
  -up: warmth that holds even when the frame is being tested in the
  same turn — which is the blend.
- The Blend: 5 — one genuine integration moment in the third exchange
  (warmth through the frame-hold, not tagged onto it). Next-band-up: a
  *capacity* for the blend, demonstrated across multiple turns and
  multiple conversations, not a single moment.
(Other criteria not exercised.)

### Sequencing checks
- Frame Control prerequisite (Composure ≥ 5): satisfied (7). The 6
  stands.
- Blend prerequisites (both poles ≥ 6, one integration moment): Warmth
  moved to 6 this session; composure at 7; one integration moment
  produced. Prerequisites satisfied; blend score up to 6 was
  available by sequencing. The 5 reflects evidence — moment achieved,
  capacity not yet owned — NOT a sequencing cap. Named explicitly to
  the student per breakthrough-handling discipline in
  `session-evaluation.md`.

### Next load-bearing gap
- Gap: The blend — integration under varied pressure.
- Leverage explanation: The moment has been produced; the work now is
  whether it repeats. The blend is the highest expression of the dual
  benchmark and the student's ultimate target; the leverage is the
  whole benchmark. The composure and the warmth are now adequate
  supports; the blend is the work.

### Next rep assignment
One real conversation this week, ideally with the senior who
collapsed you in S-015. The assignment: produce the blend moment
again, intentionally this time. Warm opening directed at the person,
then frame-hold on the term, with the warmth running through the
frame-hold. If it does not appear, that is data — note what was
different. If it appears, note what was the same. One conversation;
one attempt; observe.

### Trajectory call
- Call: improving
- Evidence basis: Composure recovered (4 → 7) from S-015's
  regression; the regression was real and is now recovered, not
  erased. Warmth moved (5 → 6) for the first time in three sessions.
  Blend moved (4 → 5) on the strength of the integration moment. The
  story of this period: the regression in S-015 revealed a gap that
  was always there; S-016 closed it and produced a moment of the
  blend. The story of the next period: whether the moment becomes a
  capacity.

### One-line character note
The student's question — "is that real?" — about their own blend
moment is the character note of the session. The people-pleaser's
corruption is to doubt the legitimacy of holding frame; the student's
doubt about whether the blend moment was "real" is the same doubt,
showing up at a higher level. The character work now is learning to
trust the capacity without inflating it — to own the moment without
claiming the capacity.

### Open questions / hypotheses for next session
The blend moment was produced in a rehearsal (coach playing the
manager), not yet in a real conversation with the manager. The
transfer is the next test. Hypothesis: if the blend moment appears in
the real salary conversation, the capacity is beginning to be owned;
if it does not, the moment was rehearsal-specific and the work
continues. Secondary hypothesis: the composure-under-authority gap is
closing but may reopen under a *different* authority (a board, a
client's CEO) — test in a later session with a new authority figure.

### Violations / self-corrections
None noticed this session. The breakthrough-handling discipline was
applied: the blend moment was named honestly as a moment, scored as a
moment (5, not 6), and the next-rep assignment tests whether it
repeats. Watched for trajectory inflation (the temptation to round
the blend score up to reflect the breakthrough) and contrast inflation
(the temptation to round up because S-015 was a regression); neither
pressure landed. The 5 is defensible against the rubric, not the arc.

### Coach state note
Coach noticed real pleasure at the blend moment — the first time this
student has produced it. The pleasure is appropriate; the discipline
is to let the pleasure live in the observation and the trajectory
sentence, not in the score. The score reflects the capacity, which is
not yet owned. The trajectory sentence reflects the arc, which is
real. Both truths, simultaneously. Coach was alert throughout, no
fatigue, no inflation pressure beyond the named pleasures.

## Arc Note (S-014 → S-016)

Read across the three logs, the arc is visible:

- **Trajectory calls:** improving (S-014) → regressing (S-015) →
  improving (S-016). The regression was real and named; the recovery
  is real and named. The arc is not a straight line, and the log
  shows it honestly.
- **Load-bearing gap chain:** warmth-receiving (S-014) →
  composure-under-authority (S-015, the regression revealed the
  upstream gap) → composure-under-authority continued, transitioning
  to the blend (S-016). The gap moved appropriately: it stayed one
  session too short on warmth-receiving (parked, not abandoned), it
  moved correctly when the regression revealed the upstream gap, and
  it transitioned correctly when the composure landed and the blend
  moment became possible.
- **Score arcs:** Composure 7 → 4 → 7 (the regression and recovery
  are both visible). Frame Control 6 → 3 → 6. Warmth 5 → 4 → 6.
  Blend 4 → 2 → 5. The arc shows a student who was improving,
  regressed under a new load, and recovered with new capacity — the
  honest shape of real coaching, not a flattering line.
- **Violation arc:** None noticed (S-014) → sympathy inflation,
  noticed and corrected (S-015) → none noticed (S-016). One violation
  in three sessions, caught in the self-check, corrected in the
  close, named to the student. Not yet a pattern; watched.
- **Character note arc:** "people-pleasing reflex is loosening"
  (S-014) → "shame at the collapse, tendency to make a skill gap into
  an identity failure" (S-015) → "doubt about whether the blend
  moment was real" (S-016). The character work is moving alongside
  the skill work, sometimes ahead of it, sometimes behind it. The
  people-pleasing is loosening at the skill level (S-014) and
  reasserting at the identity level (S-015, S-016) — the standard
  coaching pattern in which skill work outpaces identity work and the
  identity work has to catch up.

This is what three honest logs look like across a real arc. The arc
is not a story the coach tells; it is the data the logs produce when
they are filled consistently, in the same shape, every session. The
trajectory sentence in S-016's close ("improving — composure
recovered, warmth moved, blend moment produced; the story of the
next period is whether the moment becomes a capacity") is defensible
because it is built on this arc, not on impression. That is the
point of the log.
