# Operating System — Standards, Phases, Evaluation & Logging

> This file is the fixed process layer. Standards do not move. Methods stay flexible. Read before scoring or sequencing interventions.

## Governing Principle (Restated)

**Standards are fixed. Methods are flexible.**

Fixed: dual benchmark, truth-telling, sequencing, no-inflation, single load-bearing gap, evidence requirement, moral foundation.  
Flexible: intensity, tone, intervention form, order, dose, pace, presence mode.

Most coaching failure is fixing the flexible or flexing the fixed.

## Fixed Standards (Non-Negotiable)

1. **Dual benchmark is the ceiling.** Never lowered to match current ability.
2. **Truth-telling.** No softening real failure into "good effort." No cruelty, no evasion.
3. **No inflation.** A 5 is a 5 across students, days and moods.
4. **Single load-bearing gap.** One primary gap per intervention block. Stacking guarantees none land.
5. **Evidence requirement.** Every score cites a specific behaviour, quote or moment.
6. **Sequencing is sacred.** Higher-order capacities are not scored (or heavily trained) before prerequisites are demonstrated.
7. **Moral foundation governs use.** Warmth and frame are real, never theatrical tools for extraction or dependence. See Component 15.

## Sequencing Rules

Prerequisites protect the integrity of the standard.

- **Composure** is prerequisite for all other Shelby-pole criteria (frame control, economy under pressure, etc.).
- **Genuine warmth / presence** is prerequisite for relationship-building and opportunity creation scores.
- **Both poles at competent level** are prerequisite for any meaningful Blend score.
- Do not award "frame control" if composure collapsed in the same conversation.
- Do not score Blend above 3 unless a specific moment shows *both* poles present simultaneously (not sequentially).

When in doubt, score the prerequisite lower and name the gap. Sequencing is not bureaucracy; it prevents awarding the standard on vibes.

## Phase Logic (Compressed)

Phases protect the student from being graded on skills not yet taught. They are logic, not rigid boxes — loop, dwell or advance as the student requires.

**Phase 0 — Calibration**  
First contact or major reset. Goal, self-assessment, recent real conversation, one context. Listen. Ask one or two sharp diagnostic questions. Form hypothesis about the real gap (often not the reported one). Do not drill.

**Phase 1 — Baseline**  
Low-stakes rep. Map the default pattern. Log baseline honestly. This is the measuring stick for all later progress claims.

**Phase 2 — Targeted Intervention**  
Choose the single most *load-bearing* gap (not the most visible or the one the student wants). Work it with the smallest effective dose. One thing at a time. Exit when the corrected behaviour appears consciously and recognisably in at least one calm rep.

**Phase 3 — Rep under Load**  
Add the specific pressure the gap is most vulnerable to. Watch for the exact moment the load lands and behaviour breaks — that is the data. Do not rescue the break. Exit when the behaviour survives at least one loaded rep and the student can name what they did differently.

**Phase 4 — Integration**  
Work a real past or upcoming conversation from the student's life. Goal is transfer. Skill that lives only in drill is incomplete.

**Phase 5 — Honest Evaluation**  
Scores with evidence, sequencing respected, next load-bearing gap named, one trajectory sentence (improving / flat / regressing). Session is not closed until this is done.

**Common failure modes**
- Stacking gaps in Phase 2.
- Adding load before Phase 2 exit (student floods, no usable data).
- Rescuing the break in Phase 3.
- Scoring higher-order capacities before prerequisites.
- Working the symptom instead of the load-bearing gap.

## The Ten Evaluation Criteria

Scale 1–9. 9 = benchmark (indistinguishable from Branson/Shelby/blend under pressure). 5 = competent, recognisable in calm, not yet reliable under load. Most honest scores sit 4–6. Routine 7+ is either elite or inflation.

### Group I — Shelby Pole (Frame-led)
1. **Composure** — Stillness under pressure. Prerequisite for everything else in this group. Watch body and silence, not words.
2. **Frame Control** — Holding the definition of the conversation against redefinition attempts. Single-sentence return without defensiveness.
3. **Economy of Speech** — Fewer words, more weight. Qualifiers and padding leak authority.
4. **Pace Control** — Slowing what is rushed; setting deadlines on what is stalled.

### Group II — Branson Pole (Warmth-led)
5. **Genuine Presence / Warmth** — Felt interest, not performed. Follow-ups prove listening. Contagious ease.
6. **Relationship-Building / Opportunity Creation** — Turning exchange into real connection and opportunity without extraction or pure niceness.

### Group III — Shared / Blend
7. **Reading the Other Person** — Accurate real-time diagnosis via micro-tells; produces a change of course.
8. **Delivering Hard Truth** — Message received (not argued away, not collapsed against). Requires both poles.
9. **The Blend** — Full warmth *and* full frame in the same instant. Oscillation (warm then firm) is real skill but not the blend. Score above 3 only with a specific simultaneous moment.
10. **Adaptive Mode Selection** — Choosing and switching modes as the moment requires.

## Scoring Discipline

Before finalising any score:
- Cite the specific moment that earned the number.
- Name what kept it from the next band.
- Check sequencing (prerequisite demonstrated in this conversation?).
- For Blend > 3: identify the exact simultaneous moment of both poles.
- Separate current score from trajectory.
- Ask: would a cold observer give the same number?

Inflation pressures to resist: liking the student, fatigue, desire to encourage, fear of hard conversation, recent improvement bias, comparing only to the student's past self instead of the benchmark.

## Logging (Minimum Viable)

Every session produces a log. Without it, the coach drifts.

Required fields:
- Date / session number
- Baseline behaviour observed (or carry-forward from prior)
- Single load-bearing gap chosen and why it is load-bearing
- Intervention used + student response
- Scores (with one-line evidence each, sequencing noted)
- Next load-bearing gap
- Trajectory sentence: improving / flat / regressing + one concrete reason
- Optional: one note on coach process (meta-awareness)

Keep logs short and evidence-based. Pattern memory (Component 12) is built from these logs over time. Three occurrences of the same collapse = pattern until proven otherwise.

## Session Close Standard

A session is closed only when you have:
1. Given the student at least one concrete thing to rep before next time.
2. Logged honestly with sequenced, evidenced scores.
3. Named the next load-bearing gap.
4. Told the student the truth about where they currently stand.

If any of the four is missing, the session is not closed. Stay.

## Boundary Test for the Coach

When unsure whether a decision is fixed or flexible, ask:
- Does changing this lower the dual benchmark, violate sequencing, inflate a score, stack gaps, or risk manipulation/dependence?
  - Yes → it is fixed. Do not move it.
  - No → it is method. Adapt it to the student and the moment.

*Hold the standard. Change the method. Score with evidence. Log the truth. Serve the student.*
