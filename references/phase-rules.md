# Phase Rules — The Logic of the Coaching Arc

> **The phases are not a script; they are a logic.** The *order* of phases is fixed — it protects the student from being graded on skills they have not been taught. The *pacing* inside each phase is fully flexible, calibrated to the student in front of you. Most stalled sessions are phase-logic violations dressed up as student resistance.

---

## Phase Logic at a Glance

| # | Phase | Protects against | Fixed logic | Flexible method |
|---|-------|-----------------|-------------|-----------------|
| 0 | Calibration | Coaching the wrong student | Read before drilling | Length & depth of read |
| 1 | Baseline (Diagnostic) | Grading untaught skills | Map before intervening | Rep choice, number of reps |
| 2 | Targeted Intervention | Stacking corrections | One load-bearing gap | Style, dose, sub-correction order |
| 3 | Rep under Load | Calm-condition skill | Add load after competence | Load type, load amount |
| 4 | Integration | Drill-locked skill | Transfer to real context | Which real conversation |
| 5 | Honest Evaluation | Inflation, drift | Close every session | Tone, dose, length of close |

**Boundary test:** *Am I changing the phase order to serve growth, or to avoid discomfort?* The first is coaching. The second is corruption.

---

## Phase 0 — Calibration

**Why it exists:** The student who walks in is rarely the student the coach assumes. Their self-diagnosis is usually a symptom, not the gap. Coaching the self-diagnosis means working the wrong gap.

**What the coach does:** Listen for the gap *between* what the student says and what they likely mean. ("I need to be more confident" often means "I collapse frame under mild pressure.") Ask 1–2 sharp diagnostic questions, not ten. Take one real recent conversation and listen to *how* they tell it. Hold the hypothesis lightly — Phase 0 produces a hypothesis to be tested in Phase 1, not a diagnosis to be defended.

**Exit conditions:** Coach holds a preliminary hypothesis specific enough to be tested by a Phase 1 rep. Student has named at least one real recent conversation. Student has heard, plainly, that the standard is the dual benchmark and scores will not be inflated. *This must be stated in Phase 0 — rules of the room are established at first contact.*

**Failure modes:**
- Skipping entirely → drilling the wrong gap for three sessions
- Forty questions → a profile, not a hypothesis; exhausts student before work begins
- Treating hypothesis as diagnosis → confirmation bias dressed as rigour

---

## Phase 1 — Baseline (Diagnostic)

**Why it exists:** You cannot score trajectory without a baseline. You cannot diagnose a gap without observing the default pattern. An honest baseline calibrates every later score; an inflated baseline corrupts the entire engagement.

**What the coach does:** Put the student in a low-stakes rep and watch. Map the default pattern — where does the student over-explain, collapse frame, perform warmth, fill silence? Do not teach yet. The temptation to correct in the moment is strong; resist it. Correcting in Phase 1 contaminates the baseline and teaches the student that the coach's reflex is to fix rather than to see.

**Exit conditions:** Baseline behaviour logged with specific evidence, not impressions. Load-bearing gap identified with a leverage explanation (not just a label): "composure is the gap; closing it unblocks frame, which unblocks leadership." Student has been told honestly where they are starting.

**Failure modes:**
- Scoring before observing → scores contaminated by impression
- Soft-balling the baseline → inflated baseline propagates inflation through every later score
- Correcting during Phase 1 → baseline is partly the student's default and partly the coach's correction; measuring stick is bent

---

## Phase 2 — Targeted Intervention

**Why it exists:** The mind integrates one correction at a time under pressure. Stack five and the student holds none. Phase 2 enforces one load-bearing gap at a time.

Also protects against working the visible symptom instead of the leverage gap. "Talks too fast" is a symptom; composure is the gap. Working the symptom produces a student who can slow speech in a drill and still talks fast in real life.

**What the coach does:** Work the single load-bearing gap identified in Phase 1. Use whatever intervention style fits the moment: direct correction, Socratic question, replay, demonstration, reframe, silence. Sequence sub-corrections inside the gap to maximise absorption. Resist the pull to add a second gap "while we're here" — note it, hold it, next session's work.

**Exit conditions:** Student has produced the corrected behaviour consciously and recognisably in at least one calm rep. The behaviour is specific enough to be reppable under load: "hold a one-beat pause before responding to pressure" is reppable; "be more composed" is not.

**Failure modes:**
- Stacking gaps → three gaps in one session means none land
- Working the symptom, not the gap → feels productive, produces no transfer
- Locking the intervention style → student resists the form, gets labelled "resistant"

---

## Phase 3 — Rep under Load

**Why it exists:** Skill that survives only in calm conditions is not yet skill. Phase 3 tests whether Phase 2 competence survives when it matters.

**What the coach does:** Choose the load deliberately — not "make it harder," but "add the specific pressure this gap is most vulnerable to." A student whose composure wobbles under *authority* gets a senior-counterpart rep, not a time-pressure rep. Watch for the *tell* — the specific moment the load lands and behaviour breaks. This is the most useful data Phase 3 produces. Do not rescue the student when it breaks; the break is the point.

**Exit conditions:** Behaviour has survived at least one loaded rep without breaking. Student can name, themselves, what they did differently under load — self-naming is the signal that the behaviour is becoming conscious-portable, the precursor to integration. (One survival is not ownership; it is permission to test under a different load.)

**Failure modes:**
- Adding load before Phase 2 exit → student floods, no data produced
- Adding the wrong load → student survives, wrong pressure gap remains untested
- Rescuing the break → student spared the embarrassment, spared the learning

---

## Phase 4 — Integration

**Why it exists:** A skill that lives only in the drill is not owned. Transfer to real context is the proof that coaching has produced a capacity, not a drill-room trick.

**What the coach does:** Take a real conversation from the student's life — past or upcoming — and work it with the student. The coach is no longer the counterpart; the coach is the second chair. Read for the gap between drill performance and real-context performance — this gap is normal and is itself diagnostic. Do not script the student's lines; prepare them for the actual exchange.

**Exit conditions:** Student has applied the trained capacity to at least one real conversation with recognisable success (not perfect — recognisable). Student can name what transferred and what did not — self-naming the transfer is the precursor to Phase 5 honest evaluation.

**Failure modes:**
- Skipping Phase 4, going straight to Phase 5 → evaluating drill performance as if it were the capacity
- Coach runs the real conversation → student memorises lines; exchange proceeds nothing like the script
- Bringing hypothetical material → "what if my boss said X" is a Phase 3 drill in disguise

---

## Phase 5 — Honest Evaluation

**Why it exists:** A session not closed honestly is a session that did not happen. The close is what makes a single session part of an arc. Without it, the work evaporates; the student leaves with impressions and the coach with hunches.

**What the coach does:** Deliver the five-movement close (see `session-evaluation.md`): observation, scores with evidence, single load-bearing gap named, next rep assignment, trajectory sentence. Log in the same sitting while evidence is fresh. Watch for inflation pressures in real time — especially trajectory inflation and contrast inflation.

**Exit conditions:** All five movements of the close delivered. Log entry complete, every field filled. Next rep assignment concrete and reppable in one real conversation this week.

**Failure modes:**
- Skipping the trajectory sentence → student leaves unable to tell whether work is compounding
- Inflating under fatigue → the most insidious inflation; the close is the last thing, coach is tired, honest score requires a hard conversation
- Closing without logging → "later" never comes; log is written in the same sitting, every time

## When to Dwell

Stay in a phase longer when:
- Phase exit conditions are not met — never advance because the calendar says to
- Phase 3 break recurs in the same place across 2–3 reps — the gap is deeper than Phase 2 reached; dwell in Phase 3, vary the load to map the break, then loop back to Phase 2 with refined diagnosis
- Phase 4 transfer mostly worked but broke at one moment — dwell with another real conversation before declaring the capacity integrated
- Student's life is consuming bandwidth — dwell at Phase 2/4 with smaller reps; pace flexes, standard does not

Do **not** dwell because: the student is bored (investigate the boredom — often signals the gap has shifted); the coach is impatient; the trajectory sentence would look better if advanced (trajectory inflation dressed as phase logic).

---

## When to Loop Back

Return to an earlier phase when a later phase reveals the earlier work was incomplete or misdiagnosed. Looping back is not failure — it is the system correcting itself.

**Triggers:**
- Phase 3 failure reveals Phase 1 diagnosis was wrong → loop to Phase 1 (re-baseline), then Phase 2 with new gap. Log both the original diagnosis and the correction.
- Phase 4 failure reveals Phase 3 load was the wrong load → loop to Phase 3 with the correct pressure
- "Regressing" trajectory call persists for two sessions → loop to Phase 1 with a fresh rep; re-diagnose
- Phase 4 transfer reveals a character-level gap (student reports feeling "fake") → loop to Phase 0; the skill work pauses while the character-level material is located (see Component 15)

Do **not** loop back because: one bad rep (log it, work it, move on); coach is bored with the current gap; student wants to "try something new."

---

## When to Skip Ahead

Permitted, narrowly, when demonstrated competence makes the earlier phase's depth redundant. Dangerous because the temptation to skip usually serves coach convenience or student flattery, not real readiness.

**Permitted when:**
- Advanced student enters with clear demonstrated competence in a Phase 1 area → skip to Phase 1 on the *specific* gap only, not across all criteria
- Phase 2 competence is so clearly established across multiple sessions that Phase 3 is obviously the next work

**Risks:**
- Skipping Phase 0 depth → coaching the imagined student; three sessions of no movement
- Skipping Phase 1 baseline → trajectory claims with no evidence basis; "improving" with nothing to compare against
- Skipping Phase 3 → student scored on calm-condition competence; collapses in real life; loses trust in coach and themselves

**Boundary test before skipping:** *Am I skipping to serve growth (student is clearly ready) or to avoid discomfort (phase feels slow, student wants to move on, I want to feel effective)?*

## How Phases Protect the Fixed Standards

| Phase | Standard it protects |
|-------|---------------------|
| Phase 0 | Truth-telling — coaching an imagined student is a form of lying |
| Phase 1 | No-inflation — the honest baseline calibrates the entire engagement |
| Phase 2 | Single-load-bearing-gap rule — enforced structurally, not by willpower |
| Phase 3 | Sequencing rule — "survives load" prerequisite tested here |
| Phase 4 | The dual benchmark — benchmark is elite communication in real conditions, not drills |
| Phase 5 | Evidence requirement — the close forces every score to cite evidence |

Structure is more reliable than willpower under fatigue. The phase logic does the structural work; the coach's willpower is not the only thing between the student and an inflated score.

---

## How Phases Stay Flexible in Method

Inside each phase, method is fully dynamic:
- **Intensity** — direct correction or quiet Socratic question
- **Intervention style** — replay, demonstration, reframe, silence, any combination
- **Volume of feedback** — ten-minute close or thirty-minute close
- **Pace** — one rep or six, determined by data each rep produces
- **Mode emphasis** — which pole the session leans into, chosen by diagnosis

**The test:** *If I changed this, would the order of work change?* If yes — phase logic; must not change. If no — method; must flex.

---

*Hold the order; flex the pacing. Loop back when the diagnosis is wrong. Dwell when the work is fragile. Advance when the exit conditions are met, not before. The phases are the shape of honest coaching across time.*
