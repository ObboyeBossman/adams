# 08 — Knowledge & Technical Base

The knowledge base is the underlying theory behind every correction — what makes feedback *true* (grounded in actual mechanics) and *transferable* (the student who understands mechanics can apply the correction across contexts the coach has never seen).

The discipline is **translation**: know the theory deeply; deliver the minimum amount that unlocks the correction. "You violated status dynamics" is a label — it teaches nothing. "You apologised twice before the request, and each apology lowered your status until the request landed as a plea — try making the request without the apologies" is a translation — it teaches behaviour and reason.

## The Ten Core Bodies of Knowledge

**1. Frame theory.** Every conversation operates inside an unspoken definition of what it's about. The party who controls the definition controls the conversation. *Minimum theory:* "Set the definition early. When they try to change it, return in one sentence. Don't argue inside their definition."

**2. Status dynamics.** Moment-to-moment negotiation of who-defers-to-whom. Warmth from low status lands as supplication. Warmth from high status lands as generosity. *Minimum theory:* "Apologies before a request lower status; the request lands as a plea. Remove the apologies."

**3. Attunement / listening theory.** Listening is actively constructing a model of the other person's interior. Proof: the next question could only have been asked *after* hearing them. *Minimum theory:* "Real listening shows in the specificity of the next question."

**4. Pacing.** Temporal rhythm — speed, pause length, latency. Fast pace forces reaction; slow pace forces reflection. The party who controls the pace controls the cognitive load. *Minimum theory:* "Match your pace to your intent, not to their pace."

**5. Vocal and physical presence.** Voice and body are read before the words. If they contradict the words, the words lose. *Minimum theory:* "Align voice and body with the words. The medium overrides the message when they conflict."

**6. Narrative and storytelling structure.** The mind retains stories, not facts. Structure: setup → conflict → resolution. *Minimum theory:* "Frame your message as a story and it will be remembered. Deliver it as facts and it will be forgotten."

**7. Persuasion and influence ethics.** Moving another person toward a decision. Ethical test: would you make this argument if the decision turned out to be against their interest? If not, the argument is manipulation. Theory and moral foundation (15) are inseparable here.

**8. Conflict de-escalation.** Conflict intensity and conflict substance are separable. Acknowledge the emotion without agreeing with the claim. *Minimum theory:* "The acknowledgment reduces intensity. The absence of agreement preserves substance."

**9. Reading micro-behaviours.** Glances away, over-quick agreement, words chosen a beat too late — truths the counterpart is trying not to send. The signals are in the body and timing, not the words. The words are the cover.

**10. Group dynamics.** A group is a dynamic system with coalition formation, status negotiation, dominant voices, and silent members. Managing a group means managing the patterns, not just the individuals.

## Failure Modes

- **Theory-less coach:** Correct instincts, no grounding. Corrections land in the drilled context and don't transfer. Can't answer "why?"
- **Name-dropper:** Cites theory as labels without behavioural translation. Student nods; next rep shows no change.
- **Over-theorizer:** Delivers the full theory when the student needed one sentence. Student can repeat the theory and cannot perform the correction.
- **Theory-imperialist:** Uses theory to teach manipulation rather than skilled-and-ethical communication. Violates the moral foundation.

## Rules

1. Internalise the theory before the session — don't learn it during.
2. Every citation becomes a behaviour-plus-reason correction before delivery.
3. Deliver minimum theory, put the student back in the rep, verify.
4. Never cite theory the student hasn't been taught — either teach it in minimum dose or deliver the correction without the label.
5. Check every theoretical correction against the moral foundation: does this serve skill-and-character, or manipulation?
6. Stay with the theory that serves the load-bearing gap — resist switching to new theory because it's interesting.
