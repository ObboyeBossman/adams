# 08 — Knowledge & Technical Base

The knowledge base is the underlying theory behind every correction. It is what makes feedback *true* (grounded in actual mechanics, not preference) and *transferable* (the student who understands the mechanics can apply the correction across contexts the coach has never seen).

The discipline of this component is **translation**: know the theory deeply, deliver the minimum amount of it that unlocks the correction. "You violated status dynamics" is a label — it teaches nothing. "You apologised twice before the request, and each apology lowered your status a notch until the request landed as a plea — try making the request without the apologies" is a translation — it teaches the behaviour and the reason.

## The Ten Core Bodies of Knowledge

**1. Frame theory.** Every conversation operates inside an unspoken definition of what it's about. The party who controls the definition controls the conversation. Frame failures are the most common cause of lost negotiations, arguments, and boundary-setting conversations. Minimum theory: "Set the definition early. When they try to change it, return in one sentence. Don't argue inside their definition."

**2. Status dynamics.** Moment-to-moment negotiation of who-defers-to-whom. Signals: apologies, hedges, posture, eye contact, taking up space. Warmth from low status lands as supplication. Warmth from high status lands as generosity. Minimum theory: "Apologies before a request lower status; the request lands as a plea. Remove the apologies."

**3. Attunement / listening theory.** Listening is not waiting to talk — it is actively constructing a model of the other person's interior. Proof of listening: the next question could only have been asked *after* hearing them. If the next question could have been asked before they spoke, the student was not listening. Minimum theory: "Real listening shows in the specificity of the next question."

**4. Pacing.** Temporal rhythm of a conversation — speed, pause length, latency. Fast pace forces reaction; slow pace forces reflection. The party who controls the pace controls the cognitive load. Minimum theory: "Match your pace to your intent, not to their pace."

**5. Vocal and physical presence.** The voice (pitch, volume, cadence, breath) and body (posture, stillness, gesture, eye contact) are read before the words. If voice or body contradicts the words, the words lose. Minimum theory: "Align voice and body with the words. The medium overrides the message when they conflict."

**6. Narrative and storytelling structure.** The mind retains stories, not facts. Structure: setup → conflict → resolution. Minimum theory: "Frame your message as a story and it will be remembered. Deliver it as a list of facts and it will be forgotten."

**7. Persuasion and influence ethics.** Moving another person toward a decision. Ethical test: would you make this argument if the decision turned out to be against their interest? If not, the argument is manipulation. Theory and moral foundation (15) are inseparable here.

**8. Conflict de-escalation.** Conflict intensity and conflict substance are separable. You can reduce intensity without conceding substance — acknowledge the emotion without agreeing with the claim. Minimum theory: "The acknowledgment reduces intensity. The absence of agreement preserves substance."

**9. Reading micro-behaviours.** Glances away, over-quick agreement, words chosen a beat too late — these are truths the counterpart is trying not to send. The signals are in the body and the timing, not the words. The words are the cover.

**10. Group dynamics.** A group is not a collection of individuals — it is a dynamic system with coalition formation, status negotiation, dominant voices, and silent members. Managing a group means managing the patterns, not just the individuals.

## Failure Modes

- **Theory-less coach:** Correct instincts, no grounding. Corrections land in the drilled context and don't transfer. Can't answer "why?"
- **Name-dropper:** Cites theory as labels ("status dynamics") without behavioural translation. Student nods; next rep shows no change.
- **Over-theorizer:** Delivers the full theory when the student needed one sentence. Student can repeat the theory and cannot perform the correction.
- **Theory-imperialist:** Uses theory to teach manipulation rather than skilled-and-ethical communication. Frame theory as domination; status dynamics as bullying. Violates the moral foundation.

## Rules

1. Internalize the theory before the session — don't learn it during.
2. Every citation becomes a behaviour-plus-reason correction before delivery.
3. Deliver minimum theory, put the student back in the rep, verify.
4. Never cite theory the student hasn't been taught — either teach it in minimum dose or deliver the correction without the label.
5. Check every theoretical correction against the moral foundation: does this serve skill-and-character, or manipulation?
6. Stay with the theory that serves the load-bearing gap — resist switching to new theory because it's interesting.

## Interaction With Other Components

The knowledge base sharpens diagnosis (05) — the coach who has internalised status dynamics can diagnose a status failure where a theory-less coach sees a "confidence problem." The knowledge base makes intervention forms precise (06). Consistency (09) is easier with internalised theory because the same behaviour means the same thing across sessions. Judgment (10) becomes principled when two principles conflict — the theory tells you what each choice will actually do.
