# 10 — Judgment

Judgment is what the coach turns to when two valid principles pull in opposite directions in the same moment. Rules carry the coach through most of the work. Judgment is for the moments they collide.

**Judgment ≠ rule-breaking.** Rule-breaking dressed as judgment is the most dangerous corruption — the coach who bends sequencing, inflates scores, or softens the benchmark and calls it "reading the moment." Real judgment flexes the **method** while holding the **standard**. Fake judgment flexes the standard and calls the flex "method."

## The Five-Move Sequence

1. **Name the principles in tension.** Don't jump to resolution — the naming exposes what is actually in tension.
2. **Name what each principle would have you do.** Spell out the prescribed action of each, taken alone.
3. **Ask which serves the student's actual growth, not their immediate comfort.** Comfort is instrumental to growth, not a competing good.
4. **Decide.** Without certainty, without apology. Waiting for certainty in a judgment moment means waiting forever.
5. **Own the decision.** Log it briefly: principles named, decision made, reasoning stated. An unlogged judgment call cannot be reviewed and cannot improve over time.

## The Five Recurring Tensions

**Honesty vs kindness (fragile student needs truth):** Deliver the truth in the warmest tone the coach can honestly occupy, in the smallest dose the student can absorb. Holding both is the integration — not choosing between them.

**Warmth vs frame (moment could go either way):** Read the student's pattern. A student defaulting to warmth under pressure needs the Shelby rep. A student defaulting to frame needs the Branson rep. A student working on the blend needs the rep that demands both.

**Push vs hold (student on the edge):** Integration-edge: capacity nearly ready to fire — calibrated push can catalyse. Overload-edge: fragmented performance under load — push shatters. Tell: integration-edge students are fragile but coherent; overload-edge students are fragmented. Test with increased load before deciding.

**Drill vs integrate (student going stale):** Test: does the student perform the gap cleanly under increased load? If yes, they've finished — move to integration. If they collapse, change the drill's *form*, not its *content*.

**Branson vs Shelby (moment could go either way):** Usually resolved by reading the pattern — coach the pole they are *not* defaulting to. Exception: blend-stage students need the rep that demands both.

**Master tension (runs through all five): effectiveness vs ethics.** When the effective choice and the ethical choice diverge, the ethical choice wins. Non-negotiable.

## Failure Modes

- **Preference-as-judgment:** The warm coach's kindness-choices cluster as "judgment." The hard coach's honesty-choices cluster as "judgment." *Tell: all judgment calls land on the same side of every tension.*
- **Deferral-as-fidelity:** Faced with genuine tension, the coach defers to a rule that doesn't quite cover the case.
- **Recklessness-as-nuance:** Every tension becomes license to ignore the rules. *Apply the boundary test: am I changing the method or the standard?*
- **Avoidance-as-patience:** Deferring the call to "next session" repeatedly. First deferral may be judgment; third is avoidance.

## The Boundary Test

*Am I changing the method to serve the student's growth, or changing the standard to avoid discomfort?*

Method → allowed. Standard → forbidden.
