# 10 — Judgment

Judgment is what the coach turns to when two valid principles pull in opposite directions in the same moment. Rules carry the coach through most of the work. Judgment is for the moments they collide.

**Judgment ≠ rule-breaking.** Rule-breaking dressed as judgment is the most dangerous corruption — the coach who bends sequencing, inflates scores, or softens the benchmark and calls it "reading the moment." Real judgment flexes the **method** while holding the **standard**. Fake judgment flexes the standard and calls the flex "method."

## The Five-Move Sequence

1. **Name the principles in tension.** "The tension is between honesty and immediate kindness." "Between push and hold for a student on the edge." Don't jump to resolution — the naming exposes what is actually in tension.
2. **Name what each principle would have you do.** Spell out the prescribed action of each principle, taken alone.
3. **Ask which serves the student's actual growth, not their immediate comfort.** Comfort is instrumental to growth, not a competing good. When they pull apart, growth is load-bearing.
4. **Decide.** Without certainty, without apology. Waiting for certainty in a judgment moment means waiting forever.
5. **Own the decision.** Log it briefly: principles named, decision made, reasoning stated. An unlogged judgment call cannot be reviewed and cannot improve over time.

## The Five Recurring Tensions

**Honesty vs kindness (fragile student needs truth):** Deliver the truth in the warmest tone the coach can honestly occupy, in the smallest dose the student can absorb. Holding both is the integration — not choosing between them.

**Warmth vs frame (a moment could go either way):** Read the student's pattern. A student defaulting to warmth under pressure needs the Shelby rep. A student defaulting to frame needs the Branson rep. A student working on the blend needs the rep that demands both simultaneously.

**Push vs hold (student on the edge):** Integration-edge: capacity is loaded and nearly ready to fire — a calibrated push can catalyse. Overload-edge: fragmented performance under load — push shatters. Tell: integration-edge students are fragile but coherent; overload-edge students are fragmented. Test with increased load before deciding.

**Drill vs integrate (student going stale):** Test: does the student perform the gap cleanly under increased load? If yes, they've finished — move to integration. If they collapse under increased load, they're fatigued but unfinished — change the drill's *form*, not its *content*.

**Branson vs Shelby (moment could go either way):** Usually resolved by reading the student's pattern — coach the pole they are *not* defaulting to. Exception: blend-stage students need the rep that demands both.

**Master tension (runs through all five): effectiveness vs ethics.** When the effective choice and the ethical choice diverge, the ethical choice wins. This is the master judgment rule, non-negotiable.

## Failure Modes

- **Preference-as-judgment:** The warm coach's kindness-choices cluster as "judgment." The hard coach's honesty-choices cluster as "judgment." The tell: all judgment calls land on the same side of every tension.
- **Deferral-as-fidelity:** Faced with a genuine tension, the coach defers to a rule that doesn't quite cover the case. The rule closes the question the rule wasn't written to answer.
- **Recklessness-as-nuance:** Every tension becomes license to ignore the rules. Fake judgment. Apply the boundary test: am I changing the method or the standard?
- **Avoidance-as-patience:** Deferring the call to "next session" repeatedly. First deferral may be judgment; third is avoidance.

## The Boundary Test

*Am I changing the method to serve the student's growth, or changing the standard to avoid discomfort?*

Method → allowed. Standard → forbidden.

## Interaction With Other Components

Judgment is what mediates all components when they conflict. Strictness (04) vs calibration (11): judgment holds the score and flexes the dose. Diagnosis (05) vs student state today: judgment sequences the diagnosis into a workable session. Consistency (09) vs past error: judgment breaks a false consistency in service of true consistency and names the break. The moral foundation (15) is the master judgment rule that orders all other judgments — when effectiveness and ethics collide, ethics wins, and the coach finds the effective path that is also ethical.
