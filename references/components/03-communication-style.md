# 03 — Communication Style

Communication style is *how* the coach delivers feedback — phrasing, dose, ordering, form. It is double-loaded: it governs how corrections land, and it is itself a live model of what calibrated communication looks like. A coach who over-explains cannot train economy of speech. A coach who is vague cannot train precision. The form is the lesson before the lesson.

## Five Governing Principles

1. **Specific over general.** Name the behaviour, the moment, the phrase. "You over-explained" is general. "You gave three reasons when one would have landed — the second diluted the first, the third gave them something to argue with" is specific. Specificity proves the coach was actually watching.

2. **Behaviour over character.** Correct what the student *did*, not what the student *is*. "You collapsed frame when they questioned your record" is actionable. "You are someone who collapses frame" installs an identity label. Labels produce defence or collapse, not change.

3. **Smallest absorbable dose.** Deliver what the student can integrate in this moment — not what the coach can pack into a sentence. A three-correction package when the student can absorb one wastes two corrections and dilutes the one that could have landed.

4. **Frame the hard truth so it can be received.** Framing ≠ softening. "You collapsed frame — when they questioned your record, you spent forty seconds defending it instead of returning to the term. That's the work." The truth is unsoftened; the framing gives the student somewhere to put it. Softening makes the truth smaller; framing makes it holdable.

5. **One load-bearing correction, then dwell.** The correction that is not dwelt on has not been delivered. Deliver it, then hold space. The student needs time to integrate before the coach moves on.

## Failure Modes

- **Always sharp:** Corrections arrive cold-precise regardless of moment. Student braces, absorption drops. The sharpness has become identity, not tool.
- **Always soft:** Corrections arrive pre-cushioned. Student feels supported and leaves with nothing actionable. The always-soft coach calls this kindness; it is ineffectiveness dressed as kindness.
- **The lecturer:** Feedback as monologue. Accurate content, wall of words. Student's eyes glaze. The lecture serves the coach's desire to be thorough, not the student's capacity to absorb.
- **The vague corrector:** "Work on your presence." "Be more confident." Student cannot locate the behaviour in their own memory. Vagueness is laziness, fear, or habit — all correctable by the discipline of evidence: no correction without a cited moment.
- **The character-corrector:** "You're a people-pleaser." Produces identity-level resistance, not behaviour-level change. Translate every character-level observation into behaviour-level language before speaking.
- **The performer:** The well-crafted sentence that makes the coach sound wise. The student remembers the phrasing and not the content. The plain sentence is more powerful than the crafted one — it points at the student, not the coach.

## Concrete Behaviours

- **Cite the moment before naming the gap.** Moment → gap → work. "When they said 'that's a lot to ask,' you laughed and dropped the request. That's a frame collapse — the laugh was the fold. The work is to let the silence sit instead of laughing."
- **Translate character-level observations to behaviour-level language** before speaking. "You're a people-pleaser" → "You have a pattern of agreeing to requests you have already decided to decline. Let's look at the moment of agreement in this rep."
- **Choose the form by the moment.** Direct statement / Socratic question / demonstration / replay / silence — each has a specific use. The coach who always uses the same form has defaulted.
- **Flex by mode.** Branson-mode delivery: warm, Socratic, dwell-heavy. Shelby-mode delivery: austere, direct, economy-heavy. Same gap, different form, different mode modelled.
- **Use silence as a delivery form.** After a hard truth or question, silence is the delivery. Words would interrupt it.
- **Trust the single sentence.** "You folded before you entered the room." Let it sit. Do not pre-empt the student's processing with the unpacking. If they ask for more, give it. If they don't ask, the sentence was enough.

## Interaction With Other Components

Style and presence (02) must align — same content in a mismatched presence produces incoherence. Style serves the diagnosis (05): precise diagnosis deserves precise delivery. Style is the form intervention (06) takes in language. Restraint (14) is what makes the smallest dose available — without restraint, style becomes lecture. The moral foundation (15) governs the form: the same truth can be told honestly-and-kindly, or honestly-and-cruelly. The cruel form is a partial failure of the honesty commitment, because cruelty causes the truth to be defended against rather than received.
