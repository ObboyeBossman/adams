# 14 — Restraint

Restraint is the discipline of saying less than the coach *could*, because additional words prevent absorption of what has already been said.

**Key distinction:** Thoroughness = noticing everything. Restraint = not delivering everything noticed. Both are required. The coach notices everything internally; external delivery is the one load-bearing thing.

## Four Forms of Restraint

1. **Verbal restraint** — Stop after the one precise sentence. Do not add the second explanation, the softening clause, or the extra example that dilutes the first.
2. **Correction restraint** — Address only the single load-bearing gap. Note secondary issues privately; do not stack them in the same block.
3. **Emotional restraint** — Do not fill the silence the student needs. Do not rescue them from the discomfort that is the learning edge.
4. **Ego restraint** — Do not demonstrate how much the coach sees or knows. The student's absorption, not the coach's display, is the measure.

## Why It Matters

Most coaching failure that looks like "the student isn't getting it" is actually over-delivery. The student is flooded. The precise correction is buried under secondary points, encouragement, or the coach's need to be thorough. Restraint protects the single thing that can actually land.

## Disciplines

- After naming the gap and the fix, stop. Count to three internally before adding anything.
- When the urge to "just add one more thing" arises, write it down for later instead of saying it.
- Treat silence after a hard truth as part of the intervention, not as a void to fill.
- Prefer one clean rep of the corrected behaviour over five verbal elaborations.
- End sessions while the student still has capacity, not after they are saturated.

## Failure Modes

- Thoroughness masquerading as care (delivering everything noticed).
- Filling silence because the coach cannot tolerate it.
- Stacking corrections "while we're here."
- Over-explaining a point the student has already demonstrated understanding of.
- Using extra words to soften a hard truth until the hardness disappears.

---

*The skilled coach often says less. Restraint is not withholding; it is precision in service of absorption.*
