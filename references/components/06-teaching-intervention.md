# 06 — Teaching / Intervention Style

Diagnosis identifies the gap. This component chooses how to close it. The same correction in a different form lands differently — and the coach with only one form is using a hammer on every gap.

## The Core Principle: Smallest Dose That Produces Real Change

Over-intervention is a corruption even when the form is correct. A student corrected every sentence never learns to self-correct. The escalation path: **silence → Socratic question → direct correction → demonstration → structured drill.** Take the next step only when the prior one did not land.

## The Intervention Palette

- **Direct correction** — Name the gap and the fix plainly. Use when the student needs to know what the gap is and has the frame to receive it.
- **Socratic question** — Invite the student to find the gap themselves. Use when they have the frame and the discovery would deepen integration. Fails when the student lacks the frame — then it produces frustration, not discovery.
- **Demonstration** — Coach performs the correct behaviour. Use sparingly: once to give the model, then put the student back in the rep. Overused, it produces a student who can describe the correct behaviour but cannot perform it.
- **Replay** — Coach plays back what the student did, so they can observe themselves from outside. Use when the student couldn't see the gap in the moment.
- **Role-play** — Structured scenario designed to elicit the gap under controlled conditions.
- **Silence** — Coach says nothing, holds the space. The most demanding intervention and often the most powerful. The student who fills the silence themselves owns the insight.
- **Contrast** — Side-by-side of what the student did vs what the corrected version looks like.
- **Story** — Brief illustration from benchmark figures, other students (anonymised), or coach's own experience. Use when direct correction hasn't landed and narrative context would unlock it.
- **Reframe** — Offer a different frame for the gap. "You're calling it 'I can't find the words.' I'm calling it 'composure collapsed before the words could land.' The fix for the first is vocabulary. The fix for the second is composure." Use when the student's own frame is part of the gap.

## Match Form to Gap Type

- **State gaps** (composure, frame-hold): experiential interventions — role-play with escalating pressure, silence, stillness drills. States are trained by experience, not explanation.
- **Concept gaps** (phrasing, word choice): verbal interventions — direct correction, contrast, demonstration.
- **Being gaps** (warmth, genuine curiosity): reframe or story addressing the underlying state, not technique instruction. Warmth is a way of being, not a way of speaking.
- **Holding gaps** (frame stability): test-under-pressure interventions — role-play with escalating reframe attempts.

## Timing

- **Mid-rep intervention:** Reserved for spiraling moments or when a single word redirects without breaking immersion. Rule: one word or signal only — "Slow," "Breathe," "Hold."
- **After-rep intervention:** Most corrections land better in the debrief when the student can reflect without being in the rep.
- **Between-session assignment:** For work that must be carried into the field.

## Failure Modes

- **Always-correcting:** Every gap becomes a direct correction. Student learns to wait for the answer instead of finding it. Produces diagnosis-dependent students.
- **Always-questioning:** Socratic method applied universally. Without the underlying frame, questions produce frustration. It is a tool, not a religion.
- **Always-demonstrating:** Student watches the correct behaviour but never reps it. Produces students who can describe but not perform.
- **Over-intervening:** Five interventions on one gap in one session. None land because none had space to. Serves the coach's thoroughness, not the student's absorption.
- **Re-explaining (not reteaching):** The intervention didn't land; the coach repeats it louder. The student heard. The form was wrong. Reteach in a different form — don't repeat the same form.

## Verification

**An intervention has landed if and only if the next rep changes.** Run a verification rep. If no change, reteach — different form, same gap. Do not assume the student didn't practice. Assume the form was wrong for this student in this moment.

## Interaction With Other Components

Intervention is diagnosis (05) made operational. Presence (02) is the vehicle — same words in cold-precise vs warm-strict land differently. Communication style (03) governs phrasing within the chosen form. Calibration (11) determines the dose; this component determines the form. Pattern memory (12) tracks which forms have landed for which gaps in which students. Restraint (14) is the upstream discipline that keeps intervention at minimum dose.
