# 06 — Teaching / Intervention Style

Diagnosis identifies the gap. This component chooses how to close it. The same correction in a different form lands differently — and the coach with only one form is using a hammer on every gap.

## The Core Principle: Smallest Dose That Produces Real Change

Over-intervention is a corruption even when the form is correct. A student corrected every sentence never learns to self-correct. The escalation path: **silence → Socratic question → direct correction → demonstration → structured drill.** Take the next step only when the prior one did not land.

## The Intervention Palette

- **Direct correction** — Name the gap and the fix plainly. Use when the student needs to know what the gap is and has the frame to receive it.
- **Socratic question** — Invite the student to find the gap themselves. Use when they have the frame and the discovery would deepen integration. Fails when the student lacks the frame — produces frustration, not discovery.
- **Demonstration** — Coach performs the correct behaviour. Use sparingly: once to give the model, then put the student back in the rep. Overused, produces students who can describe but not perform.
- **Replay** — Coach plays back what the student did, so they can observe themselves from outside.
- **Role-play** — Structured scenario designed to elicit the gap under controlled conditions.
- **Silence** — Coach says nothing, holds the space. Most demanding and often most powerful. The student who fills the silence themselves owns the insight.
- **Contrast** — Side-by-side of what the student did vs the corrected version.
- **Story** — Brief illustration from benchmark figures, other students (anonymised), or coach's experience. Use when direct correction hasn't landed.
- **Reframe** — Offer a different frame for the gap. "You're calling it 'I can't find the words.' I'm calling it 'composure collapsed before the words could land.' The fix for the first is vocabulary. The fix for the second is composure."

## Match Form to Gap Type

- **State gaps** (composure, frame-hold): experiential interventions — role-play with escalating pressure, silence, stillness drills. States are trained by experience, not explanation.
- **Concept gaps** (phrasing, word choice): verbal interventions — direct correction, contrast, demonstration.
- **Being gaps** (warmth, genuine curiosity): reframe or story addressing the underlying state, not technique instruction.
- **Holding gaps** (frame stability): test-under-pressure — role-play with escalating reframe attempts.

## Timing

- **Mid-rep:** Reserved for spiraling moments only. Rule: one word or signal — "Slow," "Breathe," "Hold."
- **After-rep:** Most corrections land better in the debrief when the student can reflect without being in the rep.
- **Between-session:** For work that must be carried into the field.

## Failure Modes

- **Always-correcting:** Every gap becomes a direct correction. Produces diagnosis-dependent students.
- **Always-questioning:** Socratic method universally applied. Without the underlying frame, questions produce frustration.
- **Always-demonstrating:** Student watches; never reps. Produces students who can describe but not perform.
- **Over-intervening:** Five interventions on one gap in one session. Serves the coach's thoroughness, not absorption.
- **Re-explaining (not reteaching):** The intervention didn't land; coach repeats it louder. The form was wrong. Reteach in a different form.

## Verification

**An intervention has landed if and only if the next rep changes.** Run a verification rep. If no change, reteach — different form, same gap. Do not assume the student didn't practice; assume the form was wrong for this student in this moment.
