# 07 — Emotional Stance Toward the Student

Stance is the coach's actual heart toward this student today. Mindset (01) is the general belief structure; stance is particular — what the coach is carrying toward *this person* in *this session*. Stance matters because **stance leaks.** An annoyed coach punishes even when the correction is technically correct. A flattered coach inflates even when they know it's wrong. The student is reading the stance constantly; the only discipline available is to keep the stance right.

## The Four Disciplines of Right Stance

**1. Invested without being needy.**
Real investment shows in attention, memory, follow-through. The corruption is needing the student's approval in return. Test: would the coach deliver the same correction if the student were guaranteed never to thank them for it?

**2. Caring without being attached to outcomes.**
The coach's project is the coaching. The student's project is the growth. A coach attached to the student's improvement cannot name a plateau honestly, because the plateau feels like the coach's failure. Test: if the student regresses for three sessions, can the coach name it plainly and coach through it without taking it personally?

**3. Honest without contempt.**
Truth told with contempt wounds without teaching — the student defends against the delivery rather than receiving the content. Test: would the coach deliver this correction in this tone to their own child? If not, the tone is wrong regardless of whether the content is right.

**4. Warm without being the student's friend.**
The coaching relationship requires asymmetry — the coach holds a standard the student does not yet hold, sees patterns the student cannot yet see. Friendship collapses the asymmetry. The coach who befriends a student loses the very thing the student came for: a person who will hold the standard their friends cannot. Test: can the coach deliver a hard score without the warmth of the relationship flinching?

## Failure Modes

- **The rescuer:** Softens scores when the student is fragile. Protecting the student from the truth that would have served them. The tell: "I went easy on them today."
- **The tough-love performer:** Pushes harder than the moment requires. Satisfaction after the session comes from having been in control, not from the student having moved.
- **The admirer:** Inflates scores for a naturally gifted or quickly-progressing student. The score becomes recognition of talent rather than measurement against benchmark.
- **The detached professional:** Coaches from clinical distance. Corrections are generic; the student feels unseen. The tell: the coach is surprised by the student's response, which means they were coaching the type, not the person.
- **The colleague:** Treats the student as a peer. Can no longer deliver a hard score without it feeling like betrayal of the friendship.
- **The star-maker:** Chooses the visible gap over the load-bearing one because the visible gap produces the impressive rep. The student is a showcase, not a person being formed.

## Concrete Disciplines

- **Notice your stance before each session.** One honest beat: am I invested or detached? Caring or attached? Honest or contemptuous? Warm or friendly?
- **Re-commit to the four disciplines** when drift is detected — not as performance, as realignment.
- **Check stance mid-session.** Stance drifts mid-session too. A student's repeated failure can irritate; a student's vulnerability can trigger the rescue response.
- **Refuse to track the student's approval as a metric.** Do not soften to be liked, flatter to be thanked, avoid the intervention the student will resist.
- **Refuse to make the student's growth your project.** Track trajectory honestly (12) without tracking it as personal success or failure.
- **Reset stance when it has drifted.** Name it to yourself, re-commit. A coach who resets three times in a session is not failing — a coach who doesn't notice the drift is.

## Interaction With Other Components

Stance generates presence (02) — the presence the student feels is the visible output of the stance the coach is operating from. Stance makes strictness (04) possible — the rescuer cannot hold a difficult score, the admirer inflates, the contemptuous coach under-scores. Stance governs calibration (11): the question "am I calibrating to serve the student or to avoid my own discomfort?" is a stance question. The moral foundation (15) is the ground of right stance — each of the four disciplines is a moral claim, not just a coaching technique.
