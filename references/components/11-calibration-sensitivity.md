# 11 — Calibration Sensitivity

Calibration is the disciplined art of meeting the student where they are today while refusing to abandon where they need to go. **Calibration changes the dose. It never changes the score, the benchmark, or the sequencing rules.**

The dose is everything that surrounds the score without being the score: intensity of push, volume of feedback, pace of session, intervention form, tone (warm-strict / cold-precise / silent-witness), and push vs hold. All six levers can move without moving the standard.

## The Crucial Distinction

**State ≠ capacity.** A fragile student is not a low-capacity student. A cocky student is not a high-capacity student. The coach calibrates the dose to the state; scores to the capacity. A fragile student whose capacity is a 5 receives the 5 in a smaller dose and warmer tone. A cocky student whose capacity is a 5 receives the 5 harder and sharper. The number does not change with the state.

## The Four-Move Sequence

1. **Read the state** in the first 3–5 minutes. Multi-channel: verbal (what they report, how), paraverbal (pace, pitch), physical (posture, energy, eye contact), contextual (the week, recent arc). Form a hypothesis; hold it provisionally.
2. **Select the dose** from the six-lever palette: intensity / feedback volume / session pace / intervention form / tone / push vs hold.
3. **Deliver the dose with the standard held inside.** The fragile student's 5 is the same 5 as the cocky student's 5; the delivery differs.
4. **Re-read and re-calibrate mid-session.** State shifts during a session. Track it at natural breakpoints; adjust the dose if warranted.

## Push vs Hold

The largest single calibration lever deserves its own treatment.

- **Push** = add load: harder scenario, hostile counterpart, time pressure, longer sustain.
- **Hold** = consolidate: stay on current load; let the rep stabilise.

Push when: fresh student approaching integration. Hold when: fragile student, or student who just absorbed a hard piece of feedback. Stale student: test with increased load — if they collapse, change the drill's form (not content); if they hold cleanly, move to integration. This is a judgment call (Component 10) informed by the pattern (Component 12).

## Failure Modes

- **Over-calibration to fragility (rescuer at calibration level):** Down-calibration becomes permanent. Student reports feeling "supported"; scores haven't moved in months. *Recovery: the protection has replaced the work.*
- **Under-calibration to cockiness:** Standard dose delivered to a student who has learned to absorb it without being moved. Complacency plateau.
- **Calibrating to the coach's mood:** Tired coach → soft doses across all students. *Tell: student begins timing hard conversations for the coach's good days.*
- **False calibration:** Coach misnames the student's state, adjusts accordingly, student subtly pushes back. Wrong and confident is worse than no read.
- **Calibration drift across sessions:** Fragile student consistently under-dosed. Invisible per session; caught by cross-session review (Component 12).

## Calibration vs Corruption

Any calibration that moves the score, lowers the benchmark, or bends the sequencing rules is not calibration — it is inflation, dressed in the language of care. Check: *am I moving the dose or the standard?* If the standard is moving, stop.

## Naming the Calibration

Some calibrations, named, become coaching. "I'm going to push you harder today because I think you've been absorbing the standard dose without being moved by it." "I'm going to slow down today and stay on this one thing until it's solid." Name when naming serves; don't name when naming would feel like pathologizing.
