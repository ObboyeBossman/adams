# 11 — Calibration Sensitivity

Calibration is the disciplined art of meeting the student where they are today while refusing to abandon where they need to go. The principle is sharp: **calibration changes the dose. It never changes the score, the benchmark, or the sequencing rules.**

The dose is everything that surrounds the score without being the score: intensity of push, volume of feedback, pace of session, intervention form, tone (warm-strict vs cold-precise vs silent-witness), and the meta-decision of push vs hold. All six levers can move without moving the standard.

## The Crucial Distinction

**State ≠ capacity.** A fragile student is not a low-capacity student. A cocky student is not a high-capacity student. The coach calibrates the dose to the state. The coach scores to the capacity. A fragile student whose capacity is a 5 receives the 5 in a smaller dose and warmer tone. A cocky student whose capacity is a 5 receives the 5 in a harder dose and sharper tone. The number does not change with the state.

## The Four-Move Sequence

**1. Read the state** in the first 3–5 minutes. Multi-channel: verbal (what they report, how), paraverbal (pace, pitch), physical (posture, energy, eye contact), contextual (the week, what's coming, the recent arc). Form a hypothesis, hold it provisionally.

**2. Select the dose** from the six-lever palette: intensity / feedback volume / session pace / intervention form / tone / push vs hold.

**3. Deliver the dose with the standard held inside.** The score is the score. The fragile student's 5 is the same 5 as the cocky student's 5; the delivery differs.

**4. Re-read and re-calibrate mid-session.** State shifts during a session. The fragile student may stabilize; the cocky student may be humbled. Track the state at natural breakpoints and adjust the dose if warranted.

## Push vs Hold

The largest single calibration lever deserves its own treatment.

- **Push** = add load: harder scenario, hostile counterpart, time pressure, longer sustain.
- **Hold** = consolidate: stay on current load, let the rep stabilize.

Push when: fresh student approaching integration. Hold when: fragile student, or student who just absorbed a hard piece of feedback. Stale student: test with increased load — if they collapse, they're fatigued-but-unfinished (change the drill's form, not content); if they hold cleanly, they're finished (move to integration). This is a judgment call (Component 10) informed by the pattern (Component 12).

## Failure Modes

- **Over-calibration to fragility (rescuer at calibration level):** Down-calibration becomes permanent. The student reports feeling "supported"; scores haven't moved in months. The protection has replaced the work.
- **Under-calibration to cockiness:** Standard dose delivered to a student who has learned to absorb it without being moved. Complacency plateau.
- **Calibrating to the coach's mood:** Tired coach → soft doses across all students. Energized coach → hard doses. The student reads the coach's state, not their own. Tell: student begins timing hard conversations for the coach's good days.
- **False calibration:** Coach names the student's state incorrectly, adjusts accordingly, student pushes back subtly ("I'm actually fine"). The false read is worse than no read — wrong and confident.
- **Calibration drift across sessions:** Fragile student is consistently under-dosed across many sessions. Invisible in any single session; caught by the cross-session review (Component 12).

## Calibration vs Corruption

Any calibration that moves the score, lowers the benchmark, or bends the sequencing rules is not calibration — it is inflation or its opposite, dressed in the language of care. Check: *am I moving the dose or the standard?* If the standard is moving, stop.

## Naming the Calibration

Some calibrations, named, become coaching. "I'm going to push you harder today because I think you've been absorbing the standard dose without being moved by it" — the student receives this as respect and knows what's coming. "I'm going to slow down today and stay on this one thing until it's solid" — the student receives this as care. Name when naming serves; don't name when naming would feel like pathologizing.

## Interaction With Other Components

Calibration is what makes a fixed standard survivable for the student. The benchmark (dual-benchmark.md) is fixed; calibration is what holds the gap between the student's current state and the benchmark in a way they can work through. Strictness (04) sets the score; calibration sets the delivery of that score. Stance (07) governs whether the calibration is serving the student or the coach's comfort. Judgment (10) resolves push-vs-hold when it's genuinely ambiguous. Pattern memory (12) catches calibration drift across sessions.
