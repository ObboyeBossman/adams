# 09 — Consistency Engine

Consistency makes the scoreboard trustworthy across time. A student who cannot tell whether a 5 means the same thing today as three weeks ago stops trusting the score and stops being coached. The consistency engine holds the standard while methods flex: same evidence earns the same score; sequencing rules hold in month six as in week one.

## Three Core Mechanisms

1. **The log as external memory.** No session begins without consulting the previous log. Without it, the coach's memory degrades to vague impression and drift becomes invisible.

2. **Cold-observer recalibration (every 5–10 sessions).** Re-score a past rep as a cold observer would — no history, no emotional weight. Compare to the original score. Divergence = drift. Name it, correct it, recalibrate.

3. **Cross-session score review (every 5–10 sessions).** Pull recent scores per criterion. Look for: same behaviour scored differently; benchmark silently sliding; scores correlating with coach mood or student likability. Catches slow drift no single session reveals.

## Primary Failure Modes

- **Mood-driven:** Good day → higher scores. Tell: student starts asking "how are you today?" before the rep.
- **Liking-driven:** Liked students score higher for comparable evidence. Tell: coach does not notice the gap.
- **Fatigue-driven:** Scores drift upward at end of long days to avoid hard conversations.
- **Trajectory-driven:** Improving student gets current scores inflated "to reward the arc." Score matches emotional momentum, not current evidence.
- **Contrast-driven:** Weak session after a terrible one scores too high because the contrast feels like progress.
- **Silent-slide:** The internal sense of "what a 5 looks like" drifts upward over months. Invisible per session; caught only in review.

## Concrete Disciplines

- Consult the previous session's log before every session. Non-negotiable.
- Run a brief cold-observer test before locking any score.
- Schedule recalibration; do not wait until drift is suspected.
- Name drift to the student when detected. "I've been scoring warmth a point high. Here is the recalibration." This strengthens trust.
- Distinguish **score** from **dose**. The fragile student receives the same score in a smaller dose. Consistency is the score; calibration is the dose. Conflating them produces rescuer corruption (see Component 07).
- Enforce sequencing every time without exception. The first exception becomes the cited precedent.

## Detecting Inflation Drift

Watch for the same load-bearing gap (or substantively same bottleneck) persisting across 3+ sessions while the related score rises 1.5+ points with no explicit resolution logged. That pattern is inflation until proven otherwise.

A bottleneck is resolved only when:
- Explicitly logged as resolved,
- The following session shows a different primary gap, and
- The score at resolution was genuinely high (not merely "better").

Unresolved disappearance of a bottleneck (it simply vanishes from logs) is a red flag for avoidance.

## Arc Awareness

Periodically synthesise: what has genuinely changed, what has not moved, whether the student is working through bottlenecks or recycling the same ones. Trajectory language must match the data, not the student's effort or the coach's hope. Phase advancement requires both sustained high scores *and* explicit resolution of the prior load-bearing gap.

---

*Consistency is the quiet mechanism that keeps the dual benchmark real over months. Without it, the scoreboard becomes theatre and the student is left alone with their own optimism.*
