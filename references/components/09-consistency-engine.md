# 09 — Consistency Engine

Consistency is what makes the scoreboard trustworthy across time. A student who cannot tell whether a 5 means "you failed" or "the coach is tired today" stops trusting the score and stops being coached. The consistency engine is the discipline of being the same coach on Tuesday as on Friday — same evidence earns same score, same sequencing rules hold in month six as in week one.

Consistency ≠ sameness. Methods flex. Standards do not. The consistency engine holds the standards while the methods flex.

## Three Mechanisms

**1. The log as memory.** Without the log, the coach's memory of past sessions is roughly the scores on the criterion the student cared most about, plus a vague impression of the rest. The log is the external memory that makes consistency possible. No session begins without consulting the previous session's log.

**2. Cold-observer recalibration (every 5–10 sessions per student).** Re-score a past rep as a cold observer would — no context, no history, no emotional weight. Compare to the score actually given. If they diverge, drift has occurred. Name it, correct it, recalibrate.

**3. Cross-session score review (every 5–10 sessions per student).** Pull the last five sessions' scores per criterion. Look for patterns: same behaviour scored differently? Benchmark silently sliding? Scores correlating with coach's mood rather than student's performance? The cross-session review catches slow drift that no single session would reveal.

## Failure Modes

- **Mood-driven scoring:** Good day → higher scores. Bad day → lower scores. Tell: the student begins asking "how are you today?" as a diagnostic before the rep.
- **Liking-driven scoring:** Liked students score higher than unliked students for comparable evidence. Tell: the coach does not notice the gap.
- **Fatigue-driven scoring:** Scores drift upward at end of long days to avoid the hard conversation the tired coach lacks energy for.
- **Trajectory-driven scoring:** Improving student gets current scores inflated "to reward the arc." Tell: current rep score matches the trajectory's emotional momentum, not the current evidence.
- **Contrast-driven scoring:** Terrible last session, weak this session → coach scores this session too high because the contrast feels like improvement. The benchmark has not moved.
- **Silent-slide:** The coach's sense of "what a 5 looks like" has drifted upward over months. Every individual score feels honest; the cross-student review reveals the drift.

## Concrete Disciplines

- **Consult the previous session's log before every session.** Not optional.
- **Run the cold-observer test before every score.** (See Component 04.)
- **Schedule the cold-observer recalibration** — do not wait until drift is suspected.
- **Schedule the cross-session score review** — detect patterns before the student detects them.
- **Name drift to the student when detected.** "I've been scoring warmth a point high for the last month. On review, the cold observer would have scored it lower. Here's the recalibration." This strengthens trust — the student learns the coach will catch and name their own errors before the student has to.
- **Treat the past self as a colleague.** Past scores are defensible or honestly revisable. Neither infallible nor dismissible.
- **Distinguish score from dose.** The fragile student receives the same score as usual, in a smaller dose. Consistency is about the score; calibration is about the dose. Conflating them produces the rescuer corruption (07).
- **Enforce sequencing every time, without exception.** The first time sequencing is let slide, the student will correctly cite it next time they want an exception. There is no first exception.

## Interaction With Other Components

Consistency is downstream of mindset (01) — "truth serves the student" produces stable scores because the truth doesn't change with the coach's mood. Consistency is downstream of stance (07) — a stable stance produces stable scoring. Strictness (04) is per-score consistency; this component is across-scores consistency. Pattern memory (12) requires stable scoring as its foundation — pattern detection on inconsistent scoring is pattern fabrication. The moral foundation (15): consistency is integrity applied to the scoreboard; inconsistency is a form of lying.
