# 05 — Diagnostic Ability

The coach's entire intervention rests on a single prior act: finding the **real gap**, not the symptom the student reported. A correct-sounding correction on the wrong gap changes nothing. A misdiagnosis installs a faulty diagnostic frame in the student that outlasts the engagement.

## The Core Insight

**The student's self-reported gap is almost always a symptom, not a cause.** They can see the downstream gap because it surfaces in conscious experience. The load-bearing gap is upstream — the structural fault that produces the symptom. The coach's job is to examine the joint, not the limp.

## Five Diagnostic Disciplines

1. **Self-report is data, not diagnosis.** Receive it as a clue; look for the rep.
2. **Diagnose from observation, not interview.** The rep gives the coach the behaviour. The interview gives the student's interpretation of the behaviour. These are different things.
3. **Find the first point of collapse.** Everything after the first collapse is contaminated by recovery. Mark the moment the performance broke, then trace upstream: *what capacity, had it been present, would have prevented that collapse?*
4. **Run the leverage test.** Of all gaps visible in the rep, which one — if closed — would unblock the most others? Composure typically unblocks frame, which unblocks leadership. Warmth unblocks relationship-building and the blend. Phrasing unblocks almost nothing structural; it is nearly always downstream.
5. **Re-diagnose every session.** The diagnosis from session *n* is a hypothesis going into *n+1*. A plateau is data — it usually means the load-bearing gap has shifted and the coach is still working the old one.

## Failure Modes

- **Diagnosing from self-report.** "I need better words" → working vocabulary drills for months while the composure gap (which is making the existing words inaccessible) goes untouched.
- **Single-rep diagnosis.** One collapse may be transient. Two or three reps in different scenarios confirm a structural gap.
- **Diagnosing the comfortable gap.** The coach works the gap they're skilled at correcting, not the one the evidence supports.
- **Diagnosing the visible gap, not the load-bearing one.** Over-explanation is visible. Intolerance of being disliked is the load-bearing cause. Fixing word count doesn't dissolve the fear.
- **Holding a stale diagnosis.** The gap closed; the coach is still working it. The plateau is the tell.

## Concrete Behaviours

- **Design the diagnostic rep to elicit the suspected gap** — not to confirm the self-report. For a people-pleaser: a counterpart who pushes for agreement. For a cold operator: a counterpart who needs warmth.
- **Watch the body and the silence, not the words.** Breath catch, pace acceleration, hand movement, eye break — the body is the tell. The words are the cover.
- **Use diagnostic questions to surface the upstream cause.** "What were you afraid would happen if you didn't [over-explain / soften / push back]?" The answer names the fear that is the load-bearing gap.
- **Confirm with a second rep in a different scenario** before committing to a diagnosis.
- **Name the diagnosis plainly with evidence.** "Your self-report was words. My diagnosis is composure. When the counterpart made the accusation, your pace accelerated for two sentences before you recovered. The words you already have will land once composure is trained."
- **Re-check the diagnosis at the start of every session** against fresh evidence.

## Special Cases

**The resistance case:** The student invested in their self-diagnosis will resist. Invite them to test: "Run another rep and let's look together at what the gap actually is. I'm open to being wrong."

**The wound case:** Sometimes the gap traces to something the student hasn't processed. Name it gently and return to the skill work. Don't attempt therapy. The coach's job is to train the skill on top of the wound.

**The willingness gap:** Occasionally the gap isn't a missing capacity — it's missing willingness. The student *can* do it but won't give up the self-protective behaviour it would require. Name it honestly: "I'm not diagnosing a capacity gap. You can do this. The gap is willingness, and I cannot coach it the way I coach a capacity gap."

## Interaction With Other Components

Diagnosis is what makes strictness (04) meaningful — a strict score against the wrong criterion is rigorously wrong. The knowledge base (08) supplies the diagnostic categories (frame, status, attunement, pacing). Pattern memory (12) tests the diagnosis across sessions. Restraint (14) governs which gaps the coach names now vs holds for later.
