# Logging Template — The Coach's Memory and Consistency Engine

> **Every session produces a log entry immediately after the close.** A coach who does not log drifts within a month and is unaccountable forever. The log is what makes second sessions continuous with first sessions, trajectory calls evidence-based, and violations visible.

---

## The Blank Template

Copy, fill in the same sitting as the close, while evidence is fresh.

## Field-by-Field: Good vs Lazy

**`baseline_behaviour_observed`**
- Good: "Opened with a two-second pause. Returned the frame in one sentence when counterpart escalated to character. Did not ask a single question about counterpart's situation. Voice level throughout."
- Lazy: "Handled the negotiation well, mostly." ← impressions, not behaviours; unusable by a different coach

**`load-bearing_gap`**
- Good: "Warmth — specifically the *receiving* dimension. Not the lowest score, but the missing third leg of the blend. Closing it unblocks blend integration."
- Lazy: "Warmth. Need to work on warmth." ← no leverage explanation; just a label

**`intervention_used`**
- Good: "Direct correction on the frame-hold setup (one sentence). Replay of the escalation turn, twice, student produced the frame-hold themselves on rep 2. Socratic question on warmth ('what would you have asked them about their situation, if you had asked?')."
- Lazy: "Various drills and feedback." ← consistency engine can't detect if same failed intervention is being repeated

**`student_response_to_intervention`**
- Good: "Absorbed direct correction immediately. Resisted the Socratic question initially ('it felt weird to ask about them when I was being pushed'), then integrated it after a replay showed the counterpart's visible discomfort with the all-business approach."
- Lazy: "Engaged well. Good progress." ← uninformative; can't determine if intervention should be repeated or changed

**`scores_with_evidence`**
- Good: "Composure: 7 — pace did not accelerate, pitch did not rise, slight pause held before responding to escalation. Next-band-up: the pause was audible as a choice; a 9 absorbs the line with no visible adjustment."
- Lazy: "Composure: 7. Stayed calm under pressure." ← no evidence, vibes-only; the rubric explicitly forbids this

**`trajectory_call` and `trajectory_evidence_basis`**
- Good: "Improving. Composure 3 → 5 → 7 across last three sessions; behaviour beginning to transfer to real conversations (held a silence in a real meeting last week). Warmth flat at 5 for two sessions — the work that has not yet compounded."
- Lazy: "Improving." ← no evidence; a trajectory claim without data is inflation

**`violations_self_corrections`**
- Good: "Mid-session I scored Composure a 6 when evidence supported a 5, because the student was fragile. Noticed when I tried to write the evidence and couldn't produce a 5-level moment. Corrected in the close: delivered the 5, named the inflation to the student. Second sympathy inflation in five sessions — watch this pattern."
- Lazy: "None." ← "None noticed this session" is honest; "None" is a claim of innocence that proves nothing

**`coach_state_note`**
- Good: "Coach was tired — end of a long day. Caught myself softening two scores; corrected one, may not have fully corrected the other. Flag for review next session."
- Lazy: "Fine." ← no self-awareness, no accountability

## What the Log Feeds

**Consistency Engine (Component 09)** needs: score per criterion per session + evidence anchoring each score + next-band-up note (detects plateaus) + sequencing checks. Without consistent shape across sessions, the engine cannot detect drift.

**Pattern Memory (Component 12)** needs: trajectory calls across sessions + per-criterion score arcs + interventions used and student responses + one-line character notes across sessions + open questions with their resolutions.

Patterns only emerge from *consistent* data. Lazy logs produce no patterns because entries cannot be compared.

---

## Logging Violations

When the coach notices a violation (inflated score, bent sequencing, softened truth), the response is:
1. Name it internally without rationalization
2. Correct at the next opportunity, honestly
3. Log it

**Four-part violation entry:**
- The violation — what was done specifically
- The pressure — which of the six inflation pressures was at work
- The correction — what was done to correct it
- The pattern check — one-off or forming a pattern?

**Pattern check is critical:** A single violation noticed and corrected is recoverable. A pattern of violations, unnoticed, is the corruption of the coach. Three occurrences of the same violation is a pattern requiring action — re-read `coach-operating-standard.md` and Component 15 before the next session.

---

## Arc Review (Every 5–7 Sessions)

Read the last 5–7 logs in sequence. Ask:
1. Is the trajectory real? Does the call across sessions match the per-criterion score arcs?
2. Has the load-bearing gap moved appropriately? Stayed too long? Moved too often?
3. Are there patterns in the student's response to intervention that should change the intervention style?
4. Are there patterns in the coach's own violations that need addressing?
5. Is the character note arc moving toward the moral foundation?

Log the arc review as a separate entry — a short synthesis. This is what makes the trajectory sentence in the next close defensible: the coach is reporting on a reviewed arc, not guessing.

## Worked Filled Example

```
## Session Log — S-014 (date: 2025-03-14)

**Student:** M—— (six weeks in; natural people-pleaser training Shelby pole)
**Phase(s) used:** 2 (targeted intervention), 3 (rep under load)

### Baseline behaviour observed
Opened with a two-second pause before responding (new; three sessions ago they would have started immediately). Returned the frame in one sentence when counterpart escalated to character ("that's a separate conversation, this one is about what I can cover"). Held the line on the two weeks. Did not ask a single question about the counterpart's situation. Voice level throughout.

### Load-bearing gap (this session)
- Gap: Warmth — specifically the receiving dimension.
- Leverage explanation: Not the lowest score, but the missing third leg of the blend the student is approaching. Closing it unblocks blend integration.

### Intervention used
Replay of the escalation turn (twice; student produced the frame-hold themselves on rep 2). Socratic question on warmth ("what would you have asked them about their situation, if you had asked?"). Direct correction on the frame-hold setup (one sentence).

### Student response to intervention
Absorbed direct correction immediately. Resisted the Socratic question initially ("it felt weird to ask about them when I was being pushed"), then integrated it after the replay showed the counterpart's visible discomfort with the all-business approach.

### Scores with evidence
- Composure: 7 — pace did not accelerate, pitch did not rise, slight pause held before responding to escalation. Next-band-up: the pause was audible as a choice; a 9 absorbs the line with no visible adjustment.
- Frame Control: 6 — frame-hold ("that's a separate conversation") was clean, single sentence, no defensiveness. Next-band-up: earlier turn conceded slightly (framed refusal as conditional on information), giving the counterpart a hook.
- Genuine Warmth & Attention: 5 — opening warm, offer to cover something real. Next-band-up: warmth largely verbal; no question about the colleague's actual situation; attention on the request, not the person.
- The Blend: 4 — both poles demonstrated, largely sequential not integrated. Closest moment: final turn (warmth tagged on frame-hold, not running through it). Next-band-up: a single moment where both poles are fully present simultaneously.
[Other criteria not exercised by this rep.]

### Sequencing checks
- Frame Control prerequisite (Composure ≥ 5): satisfied (7). The 6 stands.
- Blend prerequisites (both poles ≥ 6, one integration moment): both poles ≥ 5 but not both ≥ 6; one integration moment attempted, not achieved. Blend at 4 reflects evidence, not a sequencing cap (cap would have permitted up to 5).

### Next load-bearing gap
- Gap: Warmth — receiving dimension (continued).
- Leverage explanation: One session is not enough to move it. Test whether the rep assignment produces movement next session; if not, re-diagnose whether the gap is upstream of a deeper discomfort with receiving.

### Next rep assignment
One real conversation this week: before negotiating any terms, ask one genuine question about the person. Not about the request; about the person. Then negotiate.

### Trajectory call
- Call: improving
- Evidence basis: Composure 3 → 5 → 7 across last three sessions; behaviour beginning to transfer to real conversations (held a silence in a real meeting last week). Warmth flat at 5 for two sessions — the work that has not yet compounded.

### One-line character note
Showing more willingness to disappoint people this session than three sessions ago — the people-pleasing reflex is loosening, which is character work, not just skill work.

### Open questions / hypotheses for next session
Hypothesis: warmth gap may be upstream of a deeper discomfort with receiving — student gives attention easily but flinches from receiving it. Not enough evidence to re-diagnose yet; test next session with a rep requiring the student to receive a compliment without deflecting.

### Violations / self-corrections
None noticed this session.

### Coach state note
Coach well-rested, alert. Noted mild pleasure at the composure improvement — watched for trajectory inflation on the composure score; the 7 is defensible against the rubric, not the arc.
```
