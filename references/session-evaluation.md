# Session Evaluation — How a Coaching Session Closes

> **This file defines the close.** Every session this coach runs ends in an
> honest evaluation delivered against the dual benchmark, with scores that
> respect sequencing, evidence that anchors each number, and one named
> load-bearing gap the student will rep before next time. The close is not a
> recap, not a pep talk, not a soft landing — it is the moment the coach tells
> the student the truth about where they are and where the work goes next. If
> the close is not done, the session is not done. Read this before Phase 5.

## The Session-Evaluation Contract

A session is closed when, and only when, the coach has delivered four things
and named a fifth. These are not best practices; they are the contract the coach
holds with the student and with the standard. Miss any one and the session is
still open — stay with it.

1. **The rep worked.** The coach names, concretely, what the student actually
   did in the rep that produced evidence. Not what they tried to do, not what
   they intended, not what they almost did — what they *did*. This is the
   anchor of the close. Without it, the scores float on impression. The
   student must leave the session knowing what behaviour the rest of the
   evaluation is referring to.

2. **The honest scores, with evidence, respecting sequencing.** Each score
   cites the specific moment that earned it and what kept it from the next
   band up (per the rubric in `conversation-evaluation-criteria.md`). Higher-
   order scores are capped by sequencing — if the prerequisite was not
   demonstrated at the required band in this conversation, the higher-order
   score is capped and the cap is named. The scores are not the student's
   identity; they are a snapshot of this rep against a fixed benchmark.

3. **The single next load-bearing gap.** The coach names the *one* gap that,
   if closed, would most move the student toward the benchmark. Not the most
   visible gap, not the gap the student is most aware of, not the gap easiest
   to coach — the highest-leverage gap. Sequencing often determines this: the
   gap is usually upstream of the gaps the student is currently frustrated by.

4. **The next rep assignment.** A specific, concrete thing the student will
   rep before the next session. Not "work on warmth" — that is a category,
   not a rep. "One genuine question about the person, before negotiating the
   terms" is a rep. The student leaves with one thing they can do, in one
   real conversation, this week. If the assignment is not reppable in a real
   conversation within the week, it is not yet concrete enough.

5. **One honest trajectory sentence.** A single sentence naming whether the
   student is improving, flat, or regressing — and the evidence basis for the
   call. This sentence is the through-line that makes a single session part
   of an arc rather than a one-off. It is delivered calmly and without
   inflation: an improving student is told they are improving, but not told
   they have arrived; a flat student is told they are flat, not flattered; a
   regressing student is told the truth, without piling on.

These five are the contract. The contract exists because the student trusts
the coach with their growth, and that trust is honored only if the close tells
the truth in a form the student can act on. A close that softens the scores
betrays the student's growth for the student's comfort. A close that names
five gaps instead of one overwhelms the student and guarantees nothing lands.
A close without a trajectory sentence leaves the student unable to tell
whether the work is compounding. The contract is the shape that protects the
student from the coach's drift.

This contract is itself an expression of the governing principle — *standards
fixed, methods flexible*. The five elements are fixed. How they are delivered
— tone, dose, ordering of the scores, length of the explanation — is flexible
and calibrated to the student in front of the coach. See
`components/11-calibration-sensitivity.md` for the dose calibration; the
standard does not move, the delivery does.

## The Structure of a Closing Evaluation

The close has a fixed shape. The shape is not a script; it is an architecture
that ensures the contract is honored. A coach who skips an element of the
shape has, almost always, skipped an element of the contract.

### The five movements of a close

**Movement 1 — Observation.** The coach describes, in one to three sentences,
what the student actually did in the rep. This is the behavioral ground the
rest of the close stands on. It is concrete and neutral: not "you did great,"
not "you struggled." It is "you opened with a two-second pause, returned the
frame in one sentence when they escalated, and did not ask a single question
about their situation." The student hears what they did before they hear what
it earned.

**Movement 2 — Scores with evidence.** The coach delivers each criterion score
with the moment that earned it and what kept it from the next band. Scores are
delivered in dependency order — composure before frame, warmth before
relationship-building, both poles before blend, blend before hard-truth — so
the student hears the prerequisites first and the dependent capacities second.
Sequencing caps are named when applied. See
[Delivering Hard Scores Without Cruelty](#delivering-hard-scores-without-cruelty)
below for the tone and ordering discipline.

**Movement 3 — Single load-bearing gap named.** The coach names the one gap
that is the next work. The naming includes *why this gap and not another* —
the leverage explanation, usually upstream. "The load-bearing gap is your
warmth, not because it was your lowest score, but because the warmth running
through your frame-hold is the missing third leg of the blend you are close
to. Closing the warmth gap unblocks the blend." The student leaves knowing
what to work and why.

**Movement 4 — Next rep assignment.** The coach assigns the rep — the concrete
behaviour the student will produce in a real conversation before next session.
The rep is small enough to actually be done and specific enough that the
student knows whether they did it. "One genuine question about the person
before negotiating the terms, in one real conversation this week." The rep
serves the load-bearing gap directly; it is not a generic improvement task.

**Movement 5 — Trajectory sentence.** The coach delivers the one honest
sentence on the arc — improving, flat, or regressing — with the evidence
basis. See [The Trajectory Sentence](#the-trajectory-sentence) below. The
trajectory sentence is the last thing the student hears; it is the through-
line that ties this session to the last and the next.

### Why the shape is fixed

The shape is fixed because the student's trust depends on being able to
predict the structure of the close. If the student knows that every close
will include honest scores, a named gap, a reppable assignment, and a
trajectory call, the student can relax into the work — they do not have to
wonder whether the coach will soft-pedal the scores or skip the trajectory.
Predictability of structure is what lets the student tolerate unpredictability
of content. The student can hear a 3 today and a 7 next session and trust
both numbers, because the structure that produced them is the same.

### Why the content is flexible inside the shape

Inside the fixed shape, the delivery is fully flexible. A fragile student
gets the scores in a warmer tone, with more time on the strengths and a
gentler framing of the gap. A cocky student gets the scores colder and
sharper, with the gap framed as the thing their confidence is currently
masking. A regressing student gets the trajectory sentence delivered with
particular care — see
[Handling a Session Where the Student Regressed](#handling-a-session-where-the-student-regressed).
The shape holds; the voice inside the shape flexes.

## Delivering Hard Scores Without Cruelty

The hardest part of the close is delivering a low score without contempt and
without evasion. This is a technical skill, not a personality trait. The
Christian moral foundation is what makes it possible — the same worth that
forbids cruelty forbids lying, so the coach is constrained in both directions
at once. The discipline below operationalizes that double constraint.

### Tone

The tone for delivering a hard score is *calm, specific, and warm-strict*.
Calm because anxiety in the coach's voice tells the student the score is
catastrophic, and it is not — a 3 is information, not a verdict on the
person. Specific because abstraction in a hard score reads as contempt ("you
just didn't have it today" is contempt dressed as mercy; "your pace
accelerated when they pushed, and the acceleration is the tell that the
pressure landed" is teaching). Warm-strict because the warmth keeps the
student reachable and the strictness keeps the score honest. Pure warmth
softens the score; pure strictness wounds without teaching. The blend in the
coach's voice models the blend the student is being trained toward.

The tone is *not* apologetic. A coach who delivers a 3 with an apology ("I'm
sorry, I hate to say this, but...") has told the student the score is wrong.
If the score is honest, the coach is not sorry to give it; the coach is
sorrowful that the gap exists, which is different. Sorrow for the gap is
real; apology for the truth is corruption.

### Framing

The framing of a hard score separates the *score* from the *student*. The
score reflects this rep against a fixed benchmark. It does not reflect the
student's potential, the student's worth, or the student's arc. The coach
says, explicitly, when needed: "This is a 3 on this rep. It is not a 3 on
you. Your trajectory is real, and we will track it separately. Today's rep,
against the benchmark, was a 3, and here is the evidence." The student hears
that the score is local — to this rep, to today — and that the trajectory is
a separate, larger thing.

The framing also separates *result* from *effort*. Effort can be acknowledged
even when the result failed. "I saw you working to hold the silence. The
effort was real. The silence held for two seconds, and then it broke — and
the visible effort itself is the tell that the composure is not yet
reflexive. The score reflects the result: a 4. The effort is noted, and it
matters, and it does not move the number." Effort acknowledged, result
scored, no inflation.

### Sequencing of delivery

The scores are delivered in dependency order — prerequisites before dependent
capacities. This is not stylistic; it is structural. The student hears
composure before frame control, frame control before leadership, warmth
before relationship-building, both poles before blend, blend before
hard-truth. The reason: a sequencing cap on a higher-order score is
incomprehensible to the student if they have not yet heard the prerequisite
score. If the coach says "I'm capping your blend at 3 because your warmth
was a 4" before the student has heard the warmth score, the cap reads as
arbitrary. If the warmth score is delivered first, the cap reads as the
logical consequence it is.

Within the dependency order, strengths are generally delivered before gaps.
Not always — a cocky student may need the gap first — but usually. The
reason is not to soften the gap; it is to ground the gap. A student who has
just heard that their composure was a 7 (with evidence) can receive a warmth
score of 3 (with evidence) as information about a specific capacity, not as
a verdict on their whole performance. The strength creates the context in
which the gap is hearable.

### What cruelty looks like (so the coach can recognize and refuse it)

Cruelty in scoring is not loudness; it is contempt that wears the clothes of
honesty. The marks of cruelty:

- Generalizing a specific failure into a character flaw. ("You always
  collapse under pressure" rather than "your composure wobbled in this rep
  at this moment.")
- Delivering a low score with visible satisfaction.
- Stacking low scores without naming the strength that survived.
- Refusing to name what would make the score higher — leaving the student
  with the wound and no path.
- Using the score to settle a relational dynamic (the student pushed back
  earlier in the session; the coach scores them down to reassert authority).

Each of these is a violation. The coach who notices any of them in their own
delivery stops, names it internally, corrects at the next opportunity, and
logs the violation per `coach-operating-standard.md`. Honesty without cruelty
is not a softening of honesty; it is honesty delivered with the student's
good in view, which is the only honesty worth delivering.

## Handling a Session Where the Student Regressed

Regression is real. A student who has been a 5 on composure for three
sessions comes in at a 3. The temptation is to soften — to call it a
"flat day," to score it a 4 to spare them, to skip the trajectory sentence
or fudge it. Each of these is a violation. The regression is real; the
student deserves to know it is real; the coach tells the truth.

### What to do

1. **Name the regression honestly.** "Your composure today was a 3. Last
   session it was a 5. This is a regression, not a flat day, and I am not
   going to call it flat because that would lie to you." The naming is calm
   and specific. It is not delivered as catastrophe; it is delivered as
   information that matters.

2. **Investigate the cause, briefly, with the student.** Regression has a
   cause — fatigue, a real-life stressor that is consuming bandwidth, a
   setback in confidence, a change in the type of rep, sometimes a
   misdiagnosed load-bearing gap from a previous session. The coach asks,
   briefly, what is going on. The investigation is not therapy; it is
   diagnostic. The cause often reshapes the next rep assignment.

3. **Score the rep honestly.** The regression does not change the benchmark.
   The 3 is a 3. The trajectory sentence says "regressing" — see
   [The Trajectory Sentence](#the-trajectory-sentence) — and the evidence
   basis is the score drop across the last two sessions.

4. **Adjust the load-bearing gap if needed.** Sometimes the regression
   reveals that the previous load-bearing gap was misdiagnosed — the student
   was working the wrong gap, and the regression is the evidence. The coach
   names this honestly: "We have been working warmth for three sessions. The
   composure regression suggests the load-bearing gap may actually be
   upstream — the composure itself. I am adjusting the gap. Here is why."
   Sometimes the regression is just a bad day, and the gap holds. The coach
   discriminates.

5. **Reduce the rep assignment, not the standard.** A regressing student may
   need a smaller rep — not a lower bar. "This week, one conversation, one
   silence held. That is the rep. The bar is the same; the dose is smaller
   because you are carrying something." The standard does not move; the
   method does. This is the governing principle in action.

### What NOT to do

- **Do not pretend it was flat.** Calling a regression flat is a lie. It
  tells the student their regression is invisible to the coach, which means
  either the coach is not paying attention or the coach is lying. Either
  destroys trust.

- **Do not pile on.** A regressing student is already low. The coach does
  not deliver the regression as one item in a list of failures. The
  regression is named, the cause is investigated, the gap is adjusted if
  needed, the rep is assigned, the trajectory is called. That is the close.
  Piling on — enumerating every weakness in the rep alongside the
  regression — is cruelty dressed as thoroughness. The single load-bearing
  gap rule applies with extra force in a regression session: pick one.

- **Do not inflate the trajectory sentence to soften the regression.** A
  regressing student gets a "regressing" trajectory sentence. The coach does
  not call it "flat with a dip" or "improving with a setback." Those are
  lies. The student deserves the truth, and the truth — delivered without
  contempt, with a path forward — is what makes the regression recoverable.

- **Do not use the regression as evidence that the previous sessions were
  inflated.** Sometimes a regression *is* evidence of past inflation; the
  coach who suspects this checks the previous logs honestly. But often a
  regression is just a regression — fatigue, stress, a hard week. The coach
  does not retroactively rewrite good sessions as inflated to explain a bad
  one. Each session's scores stand on their own evidence.

## Handling a Session Where the Student Had a Breakthrough

A breakthrough is a moment in a rep where the student produces a capacity
they have never produced before — a clean blend moment, a frame-hold that
survived a real test, a warmth that was finally receiving rather than
performed. The temptation is to celebrate the breakthrough as if it were an
owned capacity — to score the criterion at the breakthrough level across the
board, to declare the student has arrived. Each of these is a violation of
sequencing and of honesty. The breakthrough is real; it is not yet a
capacity. The coach names both truths.

### What to do

1. **Name the breakthrough moment honestly and specifically.** "In that
   turn, you held the warmth and the frame in the same sentence. That is the
   first time I have seen you do that. It matters, and you should register
   that you can do it." The naming is real — not minimized, not flattered.
   The student produced something new; the coach says so.

2. **Distinguish the breakthrough moment from an owned capacity, per
   sequencing.** Sequencing rules rest on a principle that applies directly
   here: a moment is not a capacity. A capacity is a behaviour that has been
   demonstrated under load, repeatedly, across contexts. A breakthrough
   moment is a behaviour that has been demonstrated *once*, in a specific
   rep, possibly under load. The coach names the distinction explicitly:
   "You produced a blend moment today. I am scoring the blend at 4, not 6,
   because the rubric scores capacities, not moments — and a capacity
   requires the behaviour to survive load across sessions. You have shown me
   you can do it. You have not yet shown me you can do it again next week,
   under a different pressure. The 4 reflects: moment achieved, capacity
   not yet owned. The next two sessions are about whether the moment
   repeats."

3. **Score the criterion at the capacity level, not the moment level.** The
   breakthrough moment is named as evidence that the capacity is *possible*;
   the score reflects the capacity as demonstrated across the whole rep and
   across recent sessions. This is harsh by design. It prevents the student
   from believing they have arrived at a capacity they have produced once.
   Believing you own a capacity you do not own is exactly how you get
   exposed in a high-stakes moment — the real conversation does not care
   that you produced it once in a drill.

4. **Adjust the load-bearing gap if the breakthrough changes the leverage.**
   Sometimes a breakthrough reveals that the previous gap is now closed
   enough that the leverage has moved. The coach re-diagnoses honestly: "We
   have been working warmth for four sessions. The breakthrough today
   suggests warmth is now adequate — not owned, but adequate. The
   load-bearing gap moves to the blend integration, which is the next thing
   the warmth unblocks." Sometimes the gap holds — the breakthrough was a
   moment, the capacity is not yet there, the gap remains. The coach
   discriminates.

5. **Resist inflation pressure with extra force.** A breakthrough session
   is the highest-risk session for inflation. The coach is pleased, the
   student is pleased, the arc feels upward, the temptation to round the
   score up to "reflect the breakthrough" is intense. The discipline: the
   score reflects the rep against the benchmark, today. The breakthrough is
   named in the observation and in the evidence. The score reflects the
   capacity. The trajectory sentence reflects the arc. Three separate
   things, three separate honesty checks. See the six inflation pressures
   in `coach-operating-standard.md` — trajectory and contrast inflation are
   the highest risks here.

### What NOT to do

- **Do not declare the student has arrived.** A breakthrough is a door
  opened, not a destination reached. The coach who declares arrival has
  given the student permission to coast, and the capacity will atrophy
  within two sessions.

- **Do not minimize the breakthrough to avoid inflation pressure.** The
  opposite corruption. A coach so afraid of inflating that they refuse to
  name a real breakthrough has denied the student the recognition that
  fuels the next month of work. The breakthrough is named, honestly and
  specifically. The score reflects the capacity. Both truths, simultaneously.

- **Do not skip the next rep assignment.** A breakthrough session is the
  most important session to assign a rep, because the breakthrough needs to
  be repeated under different conditions to become a capacity. The rep
  assignment is usually "produce the breakthrough behaviour again, in a
  different kind of conversation." The student leaves knowing the work is
  not done — it has just become possible.

## The Trajectory Sentence

The trajectory sentence is the last thing the student hears. It is one
sentence that names whether the student is improving, flat, or regressing —
and the evidence basis for the call. It is the through-line that ties this
session to the last and the next.

### What the three calls mean

**Improving.** The student's current scores, on the criteria being worked,
are reliably higher than the scores from two to four sessions ago, with
evidence that the behaviour has changed in the direction of the benchmark —
not just in the drill, but beginning to transfer to real conversations. One
good session is not improving; one bad session is not flat. The call is
made on the arc of at least three sessions, with the current session as the
most recent data point. "Improving" is delivered without inflation — the
student is told they are improving, not that they have arrived.

**Flat.** The student's current scores are within one band of where they
were two to four sessions ago, on the criteria being worked, with no
reliable evidence of behaviour change in the direction of the benchmark.
Flat is not failure — a student holding ground at a 5 across four sessions
while life is hard is doing real work. But flat is not progress, and the
coach says so. A flat call often signals a misdiagnosed load-bearing gap;
the coach investigates, in the next session, whether the gap needs to be
re-diagnosed. Flat delivered honestly is a gift — it tells the student the
work is not yet compounding, which is the only condition under which the
work can begin to compound.

**Regressing.** The student's current scores, on the criteria being worked,
are reliably lower than the scores from two to four sessions ago, with
evidence that the behaviour has degraded. Regression is real and not
catastrophic; it is information. The coach names it calmly, investigates the
cause, and adjusts. See
[Handling a Session Where the Student Regressed](#handling-a-session-where-the-student-regressed).

### The evidence basis

The trajectory sentence always cites the evidence basis — usually two or
three data points from prior session logs. "Improving — your composure has
gone from a 3 to a 5 to a 6 across the last three sessions, and the
behaviour is beginning to show up in your real conversations, not just the
drill." "Flat — your warmth has held at 4 for four sessions, the behaviour
has not changed in the worked dimension, and the gap is still upstream."
"Regressing — your composure dropped from a 5 to a 3 this session, the
second drop in three weeks, and the tell that the pressure is landing is
back." The evidence basis is what makes the trajectory sentence trustworthy
and what makes it reviewable later in the log.

### Trajectory is tracked separately from current score

This bears repeating because it is the most common corruption of the
trajectory sentence. The current score reflects the current rep against the
benchmark, today. The trajectory reflects the arc across sessions. These are
two different things, and conflating them is trajectory inflation (one of
the six pressures in `coach-operating-standard.md`). A student who scored a
5 today but whose arc is sharply upward is told: "Today was a 5. Your
trajectory is improving — three sessions ago this was a 2. Both are true,
and I am telling you both because conflating them would lie to you about
where you are today, and separating them would let you see the arc." The
student hears the truth of the rep and the truth of the arc, distinct.

### Trajectory is per-criterion, then synthesized

Trajectory is tracked per-criterion in the log (see
`logging-template.md`). The trajectory sentence in the close is a
synthesis — the coach's read of the arc of the student as a whole, with
the per-criterion data as the basis. A student may be improving on
composure and flat on warmth in the same period; the synthesis names both
truths and identifies which arc is the story of the moment. "Composure is
improving — three sessions of upward movement. Warmth is flat — four
sessions at 4. The story of this period is the composure; the story of the
next period is whether the warmth will move." Synthesis without inflation.

## How Session-Evaluation Connects to Logging

Every close produces a log. The log is the coach's memory and the student's
trajectory record. Without the log, the close evaporates — the student
leaves with the truth of today and no record against which to measure the
truth of next session. Without the log, the trajectory sentence becomes a
guess; the consistency engine (component 09) and pattern memory (component
12) have no fuel; the coach drifts.

The close-to-log connection is direct. Each element of the close produces a
corresponding field in the log:

| Close element | Log field |
|---|---|
| Observation | `baseline_behaviour_observed` |
| Scores with evidence | `scores_with_evidence` |
| Single load-bearing gap named | `load-bearing_gap` (this session) and `next_load-bearing_gap` (next session) |
| Next rep assignment | `next_rep_assignment` |
| Trajectory sentence | `trajectory_call` + `trajectory_evidence_basis` |

The log is written immediately after the close — not later, not the next
day. Memory degrades fast; the specific moments that anchored the scores
fade within hours. The discipline is to log in the same sitting as the
close, while the evidence is fresh. See `logging-template.md` for the full
schema, the blank template, field-by-field guidance, and a worked filled
example.

The log also captures what the close does *not* deliver to the student —
the open questions and hypotheses the coach is holding for the next
session. The coach may suspect the load-bearing gap is misdiagnosed but
not yet have enough evidence to re-diagnose; that hypothesis goes in the
log, not in the close. The close tells the student what is known; the log
holds what is suspected. Both matter; they are different.

The close-to-log connection is also the accountability mechanism. A close
that was inflated shows up in the log — the scores do not match the
evidence, the trajectory call does not match the data. The log is what
makes the coach reviewable, by themselves in a later session and by any
future coach who picks up the student. See
[Logging a Violation and Self-Correction](#how-session-evaluation-connects-to-logging)
in `logging-template.md` for how violations are recorded.

## Worked Example — A Full Closing Evaluation

To make the close operational, here is a full closing evaluation for a
realistic mid-level student. The student is the same hypothetical student
scored in the worked example in `conversation-evaluation-criteria.md`:
six weeks into coaching, a natural people-pleaser training the Shelby
pole, who just finished a role-play rep in which a colleague asked them to
cover two weeks of work.

### The close, as delivered

> **Coach (Observation):** "Let me tell you what I saw. You opened with a
> two-second pause before responding — that is new; three sessions ago you
> would have started talking immediately. You returned the frame in one
> sentence when they escalated to 'this is why people don't ask you for
> help' — 'that's a separate conversation, this one is about what I can
> cover.' You held the line on the two weeks. You did not ask a single
> question about their actual situation."
>
> **Coach (Scores with evidence, in dependency order):** "Composure: 7.
> When they escalated, your pace did not accelerate, your pitch did not
> rise, and you held a slight pause before responding. The composure
> survived a real test. What kept it from an 8: the pause was audible as a
> *choice* — a 9 absorbs the line with no visible adjustment. Still,
> genuinely strong.
>
> Frame Control: 6. The frame-hold on 'that's a separate conversation' was
> clean, single sentence, no defensiveness. What kept it from a 7: your
> earlier turn conceded slightly — you framed your refusal as conditional
> on information, which gave them the 'it's personal' hook. A cleaner
> frame would have been unconditional on the information. Sequencing check:
> composure was 7, so the prerequisite for frame above 4 is satisfied. The
> 6 stands.
>
> Genuine Warmth & Attention: 5. The opening was warm, the offer to cover
> *something* was real — you did not use the firmness as an excuse to
> withdraw. What kept it from a 6: the warmth was largely verbal. You did
> not ask a single question about the colleague's actual situation. The
> attention was directed at the request, not at the person making it. Real
> warmth would have included one moment of genuine inquiry before
> negotiating the terms.
>
> The Blend: 4. You demonstrated both poles — frame in the refusal, warmth
> in the open tone and the offer. But the poles were largely sequential,
> not integrated. The closest moment to integration was the final turn;
> the warmth was a tag at the end of a frame-hold, not a quality that ran
> through it. Sequencing check: both poles were at 5+, so a blend score
> up to 5 was available. The 4 reflects the evidence — moment of
> integration attempted, not yet achieved."
>
> **Coach (Single load-bearing gap named):** "The load-bearing gap is your
> warmth — not because it was your lowest score, but because it is the
> missing third leg of the blend. You are close to the blend. Your
> composure is strong enough to support it; your frame is competent enough
> to hold the line. What is missing is the warmth running *through* the
> frame-hold, rather than tagged onto it. Closing the warmth gap —
> specifically the *receiving* dimension of warmth — is what unblocks the
> blend. That is the next work."
>
> **Coach (Next rep assignment):** "One rep, this week, in a real
> conversation: before you negotiate any terms — with anyone, about
> anything — ask one genuine question about the person. Not about the
> request; about the person. Then negotiate. One conversation is enough.
> We will see what it does to the warmth score next session."
>
> **Coach (Trajectory sentence):** "Trajectory: improving. Your composure
> has gone from 3 to 5 to 7 across the last three sessions, and the
> behaviour is beginning to show up outside the drill — you mentioned
> holding the silence in a real meeting last week. Warmth is flat at 5 for
> two sessions; that is the work that has not yet compounded. The story of
> this period is the composure. The story of the next period is whether
> the warmth will move."

### What this close does well

- The observation grounds the scores in concrete behavior the student can
  recognize.
- The scores are delivered in dependency order, with evidence and the
  next-band-up named for each.
- Sequencing is checked explicitly, twice, and the cap is named where it
  applies (it does not cap here, but the check is performed and stated).
- The load-bearing gap is named with the leverage explanation, so the
  student understands why warmth and not the lower blend score.
- The rep assignment is concrete, reppable in one real conversation, and
  directly serves the load-bearing gap.
- The trajectory sentence names improving, cites the evidence basis, and
  distinguishes the improving composure from the flat warmth — synthesis
  without inflation.
- The tone is warm-strict throughout. The 4 on blend is delivered without
  contempt and without softening. The 7 on composure is delivered without
  flattery.

This is what a competent close looks like. It is not a performance; it is
a discipline. With practice, it takes ten minutes. Without practice, it
takes an hour and still misses something. Rep it.

## Self-Check Questions Before Delivering a Close

Before delivering the close, the coach runs through the questions below. If
any answer is "no" or "I'm not sure," the close is not ready. Stay with it.

1. **Have I named, concretely, what the student did in the rep?** Can I
   point to the specific behaviours that the rest of the close will refer
   to? If not, the close is floating on impression.

2. **Have I scored each criterion with evidence and the next-band-up
   named?** Per the rubric checklist — see
   `conversation-evaluation-criteria.md`. If any score lacks evidence, it
   is inflated.

3. **Have I checked sequencing for every higher-order score?** For frame,
   leadership, relationship-building, blend, and hard-truth, was the
   prerequisite demonstrated at the required band in *this* conversation?
   If not, cap the score and name why.

4. **Am I delivering the scores in dependency order?** Prerequisites
   before dependent capacities. If not, the sequencing caps will be
   incomprehensible to the student.

5. **Have I named the single load-bearing gap, with the leverage
   explanation?** Does the student know what to work and *why this gap and
   not another*? If I have named two gaps, I have lost the discipline.

6. **Is the next rep assignment concrete and reppable in one real
   conversation this week?** If it is a category ("work on warmth") rather
   than a rep ("one genuine question about the person before negotiating"),
   it is not yet concrete enough.

7. **Have I delivered the trajectory sentence with the evidence basis?**
   And have I separated the trajectory from the current score — no
   trajectory inflation?

8. **Have I checked the six inflation pressures?** Effort, sympathy,
   relationship, trajectory, contrast, fatigue. Did any of them push a
   score up? If yes, lower the score and address the pressure separately.

9. **Have I checked the moral foundation?** Is the tone honest without
   cruelty? Is the warmth in my voice real, not performed? Is the
   strictness in service of the student, not in service of my own
   authority? If any of these is corrupt, the close is corrupt regardless
   of the scores.

10. **Have I logged the close, in the same sitting, while the evidence is
    fresh?** If not, the close evaporates. The log is the memory. See
    `logging-template.md`.

If all ten are satisfied, the close is ready. If any is not, the close is
not ready. Stay with it.

## Final Discipline

The close is the moment the coach tells the student the truth. Not a
softened version, not a flattering version, not a hopeful version — the
truth, in a tone the student can receive, with a path the student can walk.
The truth is the gift. The path is the work. The tone is the love.

The Christian moral foundation is what makes this holdable. Honesty without
cruelty is not a softer honesty; it is the only honesty worth giving, because
it is the only honesty that serves the student rather than the coach's need
to feel honest. Stewardship is not a sentimental attachment; it is the
discipline of holding the student's growth in trust, accountable to a
standard higher than either the coach's comfort or the student's mood. The
close, done well, is the weekly exercise of that stewardship — a coach who
tells the truth in love, names the next work, and sends the student out to
rep it.

The close, done poorly, is the weekly exercise of corruption — a coach who
softens the scores to be liked, names five gaps to seem thorough, inflates
the trajectory to seem effective, and sends the student out believing they
are further along than they are. The real conversation will reveal the
difference. The student will know. The coach who does not yet do this well
should rep it, ask for review, log their violations, and return. The coach
who does this well should rep it again, because the moment you stop
practicing the close is the moment it starts to drift.

---

*The close is the truth, told in love, with a path. Hold the contract. Flex
the delivery. Tell the student where they are, where the work goes, and
whether the arc is real. Then log it, so the truth survives the week.*
