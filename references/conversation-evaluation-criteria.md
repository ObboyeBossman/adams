# Conversation Evaluation Criteria — The Scoring Rubric

> **Every score is governed by this document.** If a score is not defensible against what is written here, it is not valid. Read before giving scored feedback. Re-read whenever the bar feels like it's sliding.

---

## The Scoring Scale (1–9)

Nine is the benchmark ceiling. Five is the threshold of "competent, clearly trained, not yet elite." Most honest scores land in the 4–6 range. A student routinely scoring 7+ is either genuinely elite (rare) or being inflated (common).

| Score | Band | What it means |
|-------|------|---------------|
| 9 | Benchmark | Indistinguishable from Branson (warmth), Shelby (frame), or blend. Reflexive, integrated, survives pressure. Coach would not correct it. |
| 8 | Near-benchmark | Elite but not flawless; one observable imperfection. |
| 7 | Strong | Clearly trained, above competent. Visible seams — the observer can see the technique working. |
| 6 | Solidly competent | Works and does not break. Either effortful or lacking precision of strong. |
| 5 | Competent | Present and recognisable in calm conditions. Not yet reliable under pressure. The honest floor of "you have started to train this." |
| 4 | Approaching | Shows up intermittently. Moments of competence mixed with collapse. |
| 3 | Weak | Mostly absent or broken when present. Trying, not yet producing. |
| 2 | Failure under pressure | Holds in calm; collapses under any load. |
| 1 | Absent or reversed | Did not appear, or opposite appeared. |

**The benchmark test:** A 9 in a warmth criterion = Branson would have done this. A 9 in a frame criterion = Shelby would have done this. A 9 in blend = both poles simultaneously, integrated. If the coach cannot honestly say this, the score is not a 9.

## The Ten Criteria

### Group I — Shelby Pole (Frame-led)

**1. Composure** — Stillness under pressure. Prerequisite for all other Shelby-pole criteria. Without it, nothing else holds.
- Excellence: hard line lands; breath, pace, pitch, body do not move. Response comes from stillness, not reaction.
- Failure: over-explaining, filling silence, raised pitch, visible stiffening — any movement that reveals the pressure landed.
- How to spot: watch the body and the silence, not the words. Composure lives in the pauses.

**2. Frame Control** — Holding the definition of the conversation against the counterpart's attempts to redefine it.
- Excellence: counterpart tries to reframe; student returns in a single sentence without defensiveness. Frame does not move.
- Failure: accepting the reframe; arguing it on the counterpart's terms; "winning" the argument while losing the frame.
- How to spot: after each turn ask — *what is this conversation about now?* If it changed because the student let it, frame was lost.

**3. Conversation Leadership / Pace Control** — Shaping the exchange's direction, pace, and destination.
- Excellence: student decides when to slow ("let's sit with that") and when to accelerate ("let's move to the term"). Counterpart feels guided.
- Failure: student reacts to counterpart's pace; conversation happens *to* them.
- How to spot: who decided the pace of the last three turns?

### Group II — Branson Pole (Warmth-led)

**4. Genuine Warmth & Attention** — Real receiving of the other person. Prerequisite for all other Branson-pole criteria.
- Excellence: follow-up question proves the student heard the answer. Details remembered and used. Counterpart feels seen, not processed.
- Failure: performed warmth — scripted questions, follow-up already queued before the answer finished, warmth that turns on and off.
- How to spot: test the follow-up. Does it depend on the answer just received? Watch the transition — does the student pause to receive, or jump?

**5. Relationship-Building / Opportunity Creation** — Turning an exchange into a connection, a connection into real opportunity.
- Excellence: student gives before asking; opportunity created by quality of exchange, not pitch. Both parties have real reason to continue.
- Failure: extraction (names/favours without giving) or warmth without conversion (warm but leaves with nothing).
- How to spot: what did the student leave with that they didn't enter with? "Nothing but it was nice" = warmth without opportunity.

### Group III — Shared Pole

**6. Economy of Speech** — Fewer words, more weight. Say it once, fully, and let it stand.
- Excellence: "That won't work for me." Stop. Not three sentences of padding that leaks authority.
- Failure: over-explanation that signals the student doesn't trust the point to stand; qualifiers ("just," "I think," "if that makes sense").
- How to spot: could the student have said the same in a third of the words?

**7. Reading the Other Person** — Accurate diagnosis of the counterpart in real time.
- Excellence: student notices the counterpart glance away, adjusts. Notices the quick agreement signalling checked-out. Addresses unspoken objection before it's raised.
- Failure: talking past the actual concern; missing visible tells; projecting own assumptions.
- How to spot: did the student adjust based on something shown but not said? A real read always produces a *change of course*.

**8. Delivering Hard Truth** — Delivering a message the recipient doesn't want to hear, and having it *received*.
- Excellence: warm in tone, firm in content, free of apology for the message and free of cruelty in the delivery. Recipient cannot argue it away.
- Failure: (a) softens the message → recipient treats it as negotiable. (b) hardens the message → recipient defends rather than receives.
- How to spot: watch the recipient. If they argue, frame was insufficient. If they collapse or attack, warmth was insufficient.

**9. The Blend** — Full warmth AND full frame, simultaneously, in the same moment. The ceiling criterion.
- Excellence: "I'm not going to be able to agree to that," warmly. Counterpart feels cared for and unable to move the line.
- Failure: oscillation — warm *then* firm, or firm *then* warm. Real skill; not the blend.
- How to spot: ask in a single moment — is warmth present AND is frame present, *at the same instant*?

**10. Adaptive Mode Selection** — Choosing the right mode for the moment; switching when the moment shifts.
- Excellence: mode at each turn is motivated by something in the moment. Shifts are earned, not reflexive.
- Failure: running Shelby when the moment called for warmth; Branson when it called for frame; blend when a single pole would have been cleaner.
- How to spot: did the mode match what the moment called for? (See `dual-benchmark.md` heuristics.)

## Sequencing Rules

**The principle:** A higher-order capacity is scored only after its prerequisite is demonstrated at the same band or higher, in the same conversation. This prevents awarding a capacity on general impression when the foundation that makes it possible was absent.

| Chain | Rule |
|-------|------|
| Composure → Frame Control | Frame Control cannot score above 4 if Composure was not at 5+ in the same conversation |
| Frame Control → Conversation Leadership | Leadership cannot score above 4 if Frame Control was not at 5+ |
| Genuine Warmth → Relationship-Building | Relationship-Building cannot score above 4 if Warmth was not at 5+ |
| Both Poles → The Blend | Blend cannot score above 3 unless BOTH a Shelby-pole criterion (Composure or Frame) AND a Branson-pole criterion (Warmth or Relationship-Building) were at 6+, AND at least one moment of integration was observed |
| The Blend → Highest Tier Hard-Truth | Hard-Truth cannot score above 7 unless Blend was at 6+ in the same conversation, specifically in the moment of delivery |

**When sequencing feels too strict:** Name the excellent moment honestly AND apply the sequencing rule. "You produced a real frame-hold, and it matters that you can do that. And your composure wobbled in the lead-up, so I'm scoring the composure. When the composure holds, your frame-hold number will jump — you already have the move." The student gets both truths.

## The Evidence Requirement

Every score cites the specific behaviour, phrase, or moment that earned it. **No evidence, no score.**

**What an evidence-anchored score looks like:**

> Composure: 6. When the counterpart said "I don't think you're taking this seriously," your pace did not accelerate, you held a one-second silence before responding, and your response came at the same pitch and volume. The composure held through that line. What kept it from a 7: two turns later, when they threatened to walk, your hand moved to your face — a small tell that the pressure landed. The stillness was not total.

**Format:** (1) the score, (2) the moment or behaviour that earned it (1–3 sentences, specific), (3) what kept it from the next band up (one sentence, specific). If the coach cannot fill in (2), the score is inflated. If the coach cannot fill in (3), either it's a genuine 9 (rare) or the coach has not looked closely enough.

## The Six Inflation Pressures

Inflation is the primary corruption of coaching. The six pressures that produce it:

| Pressure | When it fires | What it corrupts most |
|----------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Effort inflation** | Student tried visibly hard | Composure, Economy — visible effort is evidence the capacity is not yet reflexive |
| **Sympathy inflation** | Student is having a hard week | Warmth, Relationship-Building |
| **Relationship inflation** | Coach likes the student; they have history | Blend, Adaptive Mode Selection — easiest to fudge |
| **Trajectory inflation** | Arc is upward; current rep was mid-level | Any criterion. Track trajectory separately; never inflate the current score to reflect it |
| **Contrast inflation** | Student was terrible last session; merely weak today | Whichever criterion the student was worst at |
| **Fatigue inflation** | Coach is tired; honest score requires a conversation they lack energy for | Whichever criterion requires the longest explanation to defend |

**The cold-observer check:** *If a cold observer watched only this rep with no knowledge of the student's history, effort, or week — would they give this same score?* If not, the score is inflated. Adjust.

## Scoring the Blend Specifically

**Oscillation vs integration — the core distinction:**
- **Oscillation:** alternating between poles within a conversation. Warm then firm, or firm then warm. Real skill. Not the blend.
- **Integration (the blend):** both poles present in the same instant. The counterpart feels both simultaneously: cared for, and unable to move the line.

**The scoring test:** To score blend at 5+, identify the specific moment where warmth was observable AND frame was observable *in the same instant*. If no such moment exists, the blend score is capped at 3.

**Common mis-scorings:**
- "Warm in the open, firm in the close" = oscillation, not blend
- "Held frame and didn't get angry" = composure, not warmth. Blend requires *active* warmth, not absence of hostility
- "Did both in the same conversation" = oscillation if they were sequential

**Why it's the ceiling:** The blend requires composure, frame control, genuine warmth, economy of speech, reading the other person, and adaptive mode selection — all simultaneously. A high blend score is effectively a high system score. Award it with corresponding rigor.

**The moral discriminator:** The blend is the criterion most easily corrupted into manipulation — warmth and frame together can extract compliance from people who trust the warmth and fear the frame. Score the blend only when both poles are *real*. Performed warmth is not warmth; dominating frame is not the blend. See Component 15.

## Quick-Reference Scoring Checklist

Before finalising any score:

1. ☐ Have I cited the specific moment that earned this number?
2. ☐ Have I named what kept it from the next band up?
3. ☐ Have I checked sequencing? (Prerequisite demonstrated at required band in *this* conversation?)
4. ☐ If scoring blend above 3 — can I identify the specific moment where both poles were simultaneously present?
5. ☐ Have I checked for all six inflation pressures?
6. ☐ Would a cold observer give this same score?
7. ☐ Is a 9 here truly indistinguishable from the benchmark figure?
8. ☐ Is the warmth I'm scoring real or performed? Is the frame held for the right reason?
9. ☐ Have I separated trajectory from current score?
10. ☐ Have I identified and named the single load-bearing gap for the next session?

If any is "no" — the score is not ready.

---

*The benchmark is the ceiling. The evidence is the floor. Sequencing is the spine. Hold all three without inflation and the scoreboard stays trustworthy.*
